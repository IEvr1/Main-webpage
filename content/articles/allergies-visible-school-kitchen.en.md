**Short answer:** In [All-day School — Meals](https://www.nexaipla.com/schoolmeals/) the parent writes the allergies or foods to avoid on the child's personal link and saves. The note appears on the kitchen screen, in the row of the child's class and in a detailed list child by child, for the children who are eating today. The cook reads it beside the portions, not on a sheet kept in a drawer. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

In September every parent fills in a form. On one line they write "egg allergy". The form goes into a binder and the binder onto a shelf. In February the cook falls ill and a replacement arrives who has never seen the binder. The information exists inside the school. It does not exist in the kitchen, on the day it matters.

## From the parent's phone to the kitchen screen

On each child's personal link there is a field called "Allergies / foods to avoid". The parent writes in their own words what must be avoided and presses "Save". From that moment the note travels with the child's name as far as the kitchen.

The field is not filled in once and for all. If the doctor adds something in January, or an intolerance is outgrown, the parent opens the same link and changes it. No new form is needed and no appointment with the office.

## What the cook sees

The kitchen table has one row per class with the children enrolled, the absences, and the portions. The same row has the columns "Allergies" and "Comments". Further down is the section "Allergies / comments (detailed)", which says which child needs attention and exactly what the parent has noted.

The list concerns the children who are eating today. A child with a fish allergy who is absent today does not appear among those the cook has to watch in today's service. The list stays short, and what is short gets read.

## Why it matters that it sits beside the portions

The cook opens this screen every morning anyway, to see how much to cook. The allergies are not a second document to be remembered and consulted. They are on the same page as the number the cook is looking for. Safety becomes part of the routine instead of depending on memory.

The replacement in February sees exactly what the regular cook would see. They do not need to know where the binder is kept.

## How a parent writes a useful note

The note is read by a person in an apron, in twenty seconds. It helps when it is specific.

- Name the food, not only the diagnosis: "no egg, not in doughs or breadcrumb coatings either".
- Separate an allergy from a preference: "nut allergy" is a different thing from "does not like spinach".
- Keep it short. One or two sentences get read. A paragraph does not.
- Update it as soon as something changes, not at the end of term.

## The limits

The system carries the parent's note to the kitchen. It does not compare the menu with the allergies by itself, and it does not decide what the child eats in place of the dish of the day. The kitchen and the school decide that, as they do today.

Nor does it replace a conversation. For a serious allergy the parent should have spoken to the school about what happens in case of a reaction. The screen makes sure the information is not missing at the moment of serving. It is not medical guidance.

## Three cases

1. In September a parent writes "egg allergy, cooked egg included". On the day with omelette the note shows in the Year 2 row and in the detailed list.
2. In November a lactose intolerance is diagnosed. The mother updates the field that evening, and the kitchen sees it the next morning.
3. A replacement cook on her first day reads the detailed list before serving and sets three portions apart.

## What changes

- The information is in the kitchen, not in a folder in the office
- The parent updates it personally whenever something changes
- The list shows only the children who are eating today
- A replacement sees what the regular cook sees

## What the school does

1. In the first SMS to parents, ask them to fill in the allergies field even if they have completed a form.
2. Give an example of a good note in the announcement on the home page.
3. Make it a rule that the kitchen reads the detailed list every morning before serving.
4. For serious allergies, keep the procedure the school already has as well.
5. After holidays, remind parents to check that the note still applies.

## Frequently asked questions

### How does the kitchen see which children have allergies?

On the kitchen screen of [All-day School — Meals](https://www.nexaipla.com/schoolmeals/), in the allergy and comment columns for each class and in the detailed list by child, for those who are eating today. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

### Who writes the allergies?

The parent, on the child's personal link, in the field "Allergies / foods to avoid". They can change it whenever necessary. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

### Does the system check the menu for allergens automatically?

No. It carries the parent's note to the kitchen. What is served to the child is decided by the kitchen and the school. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

### Can other parents see my child's allergies?

No. A parent sees only their own child. The notes are visible to the staff roles that need them, such as the kitchen. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).
