[Online Receptionist 24/7](https://www.nexaipla.com/onlinebooking/) sends an automatic SMS reminder before each appointment, and the message has a link from which the customer cancels or moves the appointment in a few taps. Those who had forgotten come. Those who cannot say so, and the time is offered again to someone else. What remains are the few who would not have come whatever you did. An appointment that did not show costs twice. You lose the takings for the hour, and you lose the customer who had asked for that hour the day before and was told it was taken. Staff are paid and wait.

## Why they do not come

Those who fail to show are not one kind of person. They are three.

The first forgot. They booked three weeks ago, life moved on, and on Thursday at three they were in a meeting.

The second knew they would not come and did not say. Cancelling needed a phone call, the phone call needed a small apology, the shop was closed when they remembered. They put it off until the hour had passed.

The third never meant it seriously.

The reminder answers the first. The link answers the second. For the third there is no technical solution.

## What the reminder does

It arrives on the mobile, where the customer is looking anyway. They need open neither email nor an app.

It is sent automatically. It does not depend on whether someone in the shop had time the previous afternoon to make twelve phone calls. The fullest Saturday has the same reminders as the quietest Tuesday.

That is the point that counts. Manual reminders get done when there is time, which is to say when the shop is empty and you need them least.

## What the link does

The customer who reads the reminder and thinks "I shall not make it" has the exit in front of them. They tap, cancel, or choose another time. They speak to nobody and explain nothing.

That small convenience changes behaviour. Most people do not want to leave you waiting. They just do not want to phone to say so.

## A cancellation is not a no-show

An appointment cancelled the evening before is not a lost hour. The time goes back into availability at the same moment, and the next person to open the booking page sees it.

In businesses with demand, those hours are often booked again within a few hours. The customer who wanted "something for tomorrow" finds it.

So the goal is not zero cancellations. It is for no-shows to become cancellations, and for cancellations to come early.

## A move is better still

From the same link the customer can change the time instead of cancelling. You keep the customer and the takings, just on another day.

On the phone, that takes a conversation. Many prefer to cancel and "ring again". Often they do not ring again.

## What a message does not solve

The customer who makes three appointments in three shops and goes to whichever suits. That one will get the reminder and ignore it.

The system charges neither deposits nor penalties. The policy towards those who do it systematically is your decision. In a small business you know them by name.

There is also the last-minute cancellation. Ten minutes before the appointment, the hour is hard to refill. If that bothers you, tell customers plainly by when you expect to hear from them.

## Measure before and after

Before you change anything, count four weeks. How many appointments you had, how many did not come without warning, how many cancelled on the day.

Most owners are surprised by the number, one way or the other. Some think they lose one in ten and lose one in five.

After setup, count the same four weeks again. The board shows you the appointments and the figures, so the second count is easier than the first.

## Details that help

The right mobile number. Because the confirmation comes by SMS straight after booking, a wrong digit shows from the first moment: the customer receives no message.

Clear service names. The customer who remembers exactly what they booked comes more readily than one who is not sure.

Phone appointments inside the system. An appointment written only on paper gets no reminder. If you make it from the page on the customer's behalf, it does.

## When the no-show is yours

There is the reverse case too: the customer comes and you cannot. If a team member is suddenly absent, the emergency reschedule on the board lets you move their appointments without letting anyone come for nothing.

The customer who came and found the door shut is the likeliest not to appear next time.

## Three Thursdays

1. A customer had forgotten her colour appointment. The reminder finds her at the office and she arrives on time.
2. A customer sees the reminder, knows he will be out of town, and cancels from the link. The hour is booked again that same evening.
3. A patient cannot make Tuesday and moves the visit to Friday himself, without calling the medical office.

## What changes in your schedule

- Those who forgot remember in time
- Those who cannot come say so without a phone call
- Hours that are freed are offered again at once
- Nobody in the shop loses an afternoon to reminder calls

## How you apply it

1. Count four weeks of no-shows before you start.
2. Put all appointments through the booking page, phone ones included.
3. Tell customers that they cancel and change from the message.
4. Decide by when you consider a cancellation timely.
5. Count again after four weeks.
