> **Quick answer:** From the link that comes with the confirmation SMS of [Online Food Orders](https://www.nexaipla.com/foodorder/). The customer opens it in the phone's browser, with no app and no password, and sees the order and its status. From the same page they modify it, cancel it, or see their history. For delivery they also receive a second SMS with an estimated arrival time when the driver sets off. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://nexaipla.com) (nexaipla.com).

Waiting for food has two parts: the time that passes and the uncertainty. The shop cannot always shorten the time. It can bring the uncertainty to zero, and that is usually what makes the customer pick up the phone.

## Three questions every customer has

Did you get it? This one comes in the first minute. The customer pressed a button on a web page and does not know whether anyone at the other end saw it.

Can I change something? This one comes in the third minute, when they remember that they forgot the soft drink or that one of the group is not coming.

When does it arrive? This one comes in the twentieth minute, and it is the most impatient.

On the phone, each of these is a call. With the link, none is.

## The first SMS

A few seconds after sending, the customer has a message on their phone. That answers the first question: the order was registered.

The message contains a link that belongs to this one order alone. The customer does not need to remember an order number or log in anywhere. They tap the link and see their own order.

## What is behind the link

The order as it was sent: dishes, quantities, notes, pickup or delivery, time. If something was entered wrongly, they see it themselves before they see it on the plate.

The status. The customer sees what point the order has reached, as the shop moves it along. For delivery this includes the moment it became ready and the moment it left.

The actions. Modify and cancel, by the customer. That answers the second question without the kitchen being disturbed: the change appears straight in the shop's queue.

The history. Their previous orders, for anyone who wants to recall what they had last time.

## The second SMS

For delivery there is a second message. When the driver takes the bag and declares that they have set off, the customer receives an SMS with the estimated arrival time.

That answers the third question before it can be asked. The customer does not need to refresh any page. The message comes by itself, at the moment it has something to say.

For pickup there is no driver, so no second message either. The customer sees the status from the link and calls in at the shop.

## Why SMS and not an app

An app notification assumes that the customer installed something, gave permission for notifications, and did not switch them off. An SMS assumes nothing. It reaches an old phone, a visitor's phone, a phone with no free space.

And it stays. A week later the customer finds the message, opens the link, and sees what they had ordered.

## What it does not show

Tracking shows stages, not a dot on a map. The system does not locate the driver. The customer learns that the order has left and roughly when it arrives, not which street the moped is on.

For most customers that is enough. What they are asking for is certainty, not a show.

## From the shop's side

The status the customer sees is only as accurate as the staff's taps. If the kitchen does not mark the order ready, or the driver declares "on the way" halfway through the trip, the customer sees an old picture.

So it is worth telling staff this: every tap you make is a phone call you will not receive.

It is also worth telling the customer. One line on the packaging or on your page, "track your order from the SMS", teaches regulars to look at the message before picking up the phone.

## Three customers

1. A customer forgets to add a salad. She opens the link two minutes later and adds it. The card in the kitchen updates.
2. A customer in an office receives the arrival SMS and goes down to the building entrance, instead of the driver hunting for the floor.
3. A group changes plans. They cancel from the link before the food is prepared, with no awkward phone call.

## What customer and shop gain

- The customer knows at once that the order was registered
- Changes are made in writing and reach the kitchen with no go-between
- The arrival message comes without anyone asking for it
- The till receives fewer tracking calls

## How you make use of it

1. Place a test order and open the link, to see what the customer sees.
2. Agree who marks the order ready and when.
3. Ask drivers to declare departure before they leave.
4. Write on the packaging that tracking is done from the SMS.
5. Count the "where is my order" calls before and after.
