> **Quick answer:** Yes. In [Online Food Orders](https://www.nexaipla.com/foodorder/) the customer creates no account and sets no password. They fill the cart, choose pickup or delivery, and give a mobile number. The confirmation SMS contains a personal link from which they change or cancel the order, track it, and see their history. The link plays the part of the account. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://nexaipla.com) (nexaipla.com).

Nobody wants a relationship with a souvlaki shop. They want two pittas. The sign-up screen asks someone who is hungry to think of a password with a capital and a symbol, to confirm an email, and to remember later which email they gave. Many stop there, and the shop never learns that it lost them.

## Why most systems ask for an account

An account does three jobs. It identifies the customer, so that the shop knows whom to notify. It gives them a way to see and change their order. And it keeps their history.

Those three jobs are useful. A password, though, is not the only way to get them done.

## How the same jobs are done without a password

Identification is done by the mobile number. The customer gives it at the end of the order, just as they would say it on the phone. The system accepts European mobile numbers, so visitors too.

Managing is done by the link in the SMS. It is personal to that particular order. Whoever holds it can see the order, modify it, cancel it, and track it. Since it was sent to the mobile the customer declared, only they have it.

The history is visible from the same link. The customer sees what they have ordered, without logging in anywhere.

## The customer's route

They open the page from a link or a QR code. They see the menu in Greek or in English. They add dishes, write a note in the cart, choose pickup or delivery and, if they wish, a later time. They type their mobile number. They send.

There is no "confirm your email" step and no "the password must have eight characters". Between the first look at the menu and the SMS lie only as many dishes as they want to choose.

## What the shop stores

Less than a system with accounts would keep. There are no customer passwords to leak, because there are no passwords. There are the orders, the mobile number and, for delivery, the drop-off point.

Each restaurant runs on its own deployment, with its data separate from every other shop. Your customer does not become a member of a platform that will send them offers from competitors.

## The second order

Next time the customer does not "log in". They open the page again, choose, and give the same number. There is no forgotten password, no "your account has been locked" message, and no reset email that lands in spam. For a neighbourhood shop this matters more than it seems: the customer who orders once a month is exactly the one who never remembers their password.

## The objections we hear

"Without an account we will get fake orders." The order is tied to a real mobile number that receives an SMS. That is the same level of certainty you have on the phone today, except that the number is written down.

"The customer will lose the SMS." The SMS stays in their messages, beside the others. And if something goes wrong, the shop's phone is always there.

"Without accounts we build no customer base." The owner's dashboard shows the orders and the shop's key figures. The question is which you prefer: a hundred accounts or a hundred and forty orders.

## Where it fits best

In shops where the order is an impulse: souvlaki, pizza, burgers, coffee, breakfast. In tourist areas, where the customer will order twice in a week and never come back. And in neighbourhoods with older customers, who would not make an account but know how to open a message.

## Three customers

1. A tourist scans the QR code on the door, orders from the English menu with a foreign mobile, and collects in twenty minutes.
2. A customer who orders every Friday opens last week's SMS, sees what she had, and orders again.
3. A father remembers that his child does not eat peppers, opens the link, and changes the order before it goes into the oven.

## What the shop gains

- Fewer customers abandon the cart at the last step
- There are no customer passwords to guard
- Visitors and passers-by order just as regulars do
- Changes and cancellations are made by the customer, not on the phone

## How you try it

1. Open the ordering page on a phone that has never visited it.
2. Place a test order and time how long it takes until the SMS.
3. Open the link in the SMS and change one dish.
4. See how the change showed on the kitchen dashboard.
5. Hand the phone to someone who does not know the system and watch where they get stuck.
