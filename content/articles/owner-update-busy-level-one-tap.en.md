> **Quick answer:** The owner updates the busy level from a private screen in [Live Business Traffic](https://www.nexaipla.com/shoptraffic/) that has four colour-matched buttons: LOW, MEDIUM, BUSY, VERY BUSY. One tap changes what customers see on the public page. The screen opens from a secure personal link, with no shared password, and can be added to the phone's home screen. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://nexaipla.com) (nexaipla.com).

Every owner asks the same thing on first seeing a live busy page: "And who is going to update it? On Saturdays I am alone in here." It is the right question. A page that says LOW while eight people are waiting is worse than no page. So the update has to cost almost nothing, and it has to become a reflex.

## The owner screen

The private screen is built around four large buttons, one per level, in the same colours the customer sees. You tap the one that matches the room and go back to work. There is no form, no save button, and no text to type. The public page picks up the new level by itself, because it refreshes automatically.

Because the buttons are large and coloured, you do not need to read them. After a day or two your thumb goes to the right corner on its own, the way it finds the light switch at home.

The same private dashboard holds the less frequent settings: weekly hours, closed days, the announcement, and an emergency open or close for the moment something unexpected happens.

## A link instead of a password

You reach the screen through a secure link that is personal to you. There is no username and no password for the staff to share, write on a sticky note, or forget. On phones that support it the page installs as an icon on the home screen and opens with one touch. It works on any phone or tablet with a browser, so the shop needs no new equipment.

Treat the link like a key. Give it to the people who should be able to change the level and to nobody else. If a phone is lost or someone leaves the team, ask NexAIpla for a new link so that the old one stops working.

## Turning the tap into a habit

The rule "update it when it changes" does not survive a full day. Tie the tap to something your hands already do.

- At the barber's chair: every time a customer stands up.
- At the till: every time the line reaches or leaves a fixed mark, such as the end of the counter.
- In a café: when the last free table is taken, and when two are free again.

Add two fixed taps that depend on nothing: one when you open and one before each known peak. Starting the day on the right colour matters, because yesterday's last level is not today's truth.

## When the level is wrong

It will be, sometimes. The answer is to let your mistakes lean in the customer's favour. If you hesitate between two levels, choose the busier one. A customer who expected BUSY and found a short wait is pleased. A customer who expected LOW and found a queue stops trusting the page.

If a rush truly leaves no free hand for half an hour, tap VERY BUSY at the start and leave it. That is accurate enough.

## How three shops do it

1. A barber who works alone keeps the phone on a stand beside the mirror. He taps with a knuckle while shaking out the cape, without unlocking anything else.
2. In a pharmacy with three staff, the level belongs to the till. The pharmacist never touches it.
3. A café owner taps BUSY at 08:00 when the commuters arrive, and has a daily alarm at 10:30 that reminds her to go back to LOW.

## What the person at the counter gains

- An update is one tap, with no typing
- No shared password written on a sticky note
- The same screen holds hours, closed days, and the emergency close
- A wrong level is corrected in a second

## From day one

1. Open the owner link and add it to the home screen of the phone or tablet that stays in the shop.
2. Choose the physical action that will trigger the tap.
3. Name one owner of the level per shift.
4. Put the opening tap and the pre-peak tap into the daily routine.
5. After a week, ask three regulars whether the colour matched what they found.
