> **Quick answer:** Whatever you decide. The reply from [Custom AI Tools](https://www.nexaipla.com/ai/) creates no obligation. You can stop there, ask for clarification on the percentage, the cost, or the time, or go ahead following the recommended next steps, which usually mean starting with a small, measurable piece of the job. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://nexaipla.com) (nexaipla.com).

The reply has arrived. It has a percentage, a range, an estimate in weeks, and some suggestions. The next move is yours, and there are three doors.

## First door: nothing

It is a perfectly legitimate choice. Perhaps the cost is above what the problem is worth to you. Perhaps feasibility was low. Perhaps it is simply not the season.

Keep the reply. Conditions change: the business grows, the task becomes more frequent, the technology matures. What does not pay this year may pay next year.

And you have learned something. You now know the order of magnitude, and the next time someone offers you an "AI solution" you have a yardstick.

## Second door: questions

The reply is short by design. It is natural for it to leave questions.

Why this percentage and not a higher one? What would have to change for it to rise?

What puts me at the lower end of the cost range and what at the upper?

What will you need from me, and how much of my own time?

Can it be done in stages, so that I pay for the first and see?

What happens if it does not work as we expected?

None of these commits you. Their answers often change the decision, one way or the other.

## Third door: we go ahead

If you decide to continue, the first step is to fill the gaps the description left.

A few paragraphs do not show the exceptions. The customer who always sends the order as a photograph. The document that comes in two versions. At this stage we look together at real examples of the work.

With those, the indicative range narrows to a specific proposal. You see exactly what will be done, what it costs, and when, before you commit to anything.

## Start with one piece

The recommended next steps almost always point to something small.

Not "all the documents", but one type. Not "all the channels", but email. Not all three shops, but one.

The reason is not caution. It is that a small piece shows in two or three weeks what a large project would show in three months: whether the tool fits the way you work.

If it fits, you extend it with knowledge. If not, you have lost little.

## A trial with real work

A tool that works in a demonstration has proved nothing. The trial that counts is done with Tuesday's real orders, by the person who will use it.

Keep notes during that period. Where it got things wrong. Where it was slower than doing it by hand. What nobody had foreseen. Those notes are worth more than any specification.

## What will be needed from you

Time from the person who knows the job. Not much, but regular. That is usually the scarcest material.

Examples. The documents, the messages, the cases as they really are, supplied in the way we shall have agreed.

A decision on who will use it and who will be responsible inside the business.

And access to the programs it must connect to, if any.

## How to read the reply in order to decide

The feasibility percentage with a condition: can you meet the condition? If it says "provided orders arrive in writing" and half come by phone, the first job is there.

The cost range: set it against what the task costs you today in hours and mistakes. The method is in [how the return is calculated](/en/articles/calculate-automation-roi).

The time: is there a season when you must not change anything? A shop does not try out a new tool in December.

## When the recommendation is a ready-made application

Sometimes the next steps say that you do not need a bespoke project. If the need is appointments, for instance, [Online Receptionist 24/7](https://www.nexaipla.com/onlinebooking/) already exists.

In that case the "afterwards" is simpler and faster: you look at the application's page and decide.

## When the recommendation is "prepare first"

At other times the reply says something must come first. The records gathered in one place. The rules that today are only in your head written down. A process that changes every week made steady.

That work is yours, and benefits the business even if no automation is ever done. When it is complete, you describe the need again, and the percentage will be different.

## Two traps on your own side

Wanting everything at once. The success of the first piece creates an appetite for ten more. One at a time.

The project having no owner inside the business. A tool that "belongs to everyone" is looked after by no one.

## How much time to give the decision

A week is reasonable. It is enough to discuss it with whoever you must and to put your questions. If three months pass, the reply has probably aged, and it is better to ask again.

## Three continuations

1. A wholesaler receives high feasibility, asks what puts him at the lower end of the cost, and starts with the orders from a single channel.
2. The owner of a tutoring centre reads that the cost exceeds the benefit at her volume, and does not go ahead. She keeps the reply for next year.
3. A technical firm receives a recommendation to organise its files first. It does so in two months and describes the need again.

## How you make use of the reply

1. Read the reasoning behind the percentage, not only the number.
2. Write down your questions and send them.
3. Decide who in the business will be responsible.
4. If you go ahead, start with the smallest useful piece.
5. Try it with real work and note what did not go well.
