> **Quick answer:** By opening the kitchen screen in [All-day School — Meals](https://www.nexaipla.com/schoolmeals/). There the cook sees, for each class, enrolled children, absences, and portions, the day's total and, underneath, a detailed list of allergies for only the children who are eating today. The numbers change as soon as a parent reports an absence. Access is by a secret link and the kitchen PIN. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://nexaipla.com) (nexaipla.com).

The cook has two questions every morning. How many plates, and which plates need care. In most schools the two answers come from different places: the number from a phone call and the allergies from a sheet on the wall. The trouble is that the two lists do not talk to each other.

## The portions table

The upper part of the screen is a table with one row for each class. There are five columns: Enrolled, Absences, Portions, Allergies, Comments. The last row is the Total.

The column that gets cooked is Portions: enrolled minus absences. The Allergies column beside it shows which classes have something to watch, before the eye even moves down to the list.

The cook sees no parents' phone numbers and no contact details. The screen holds what is needed to cook and to serve.

## The allergies list

Under the table is the detailed list: the children with a declared allergy, with the name, the class, and the text the parent wrote.

The detail that makes the difference is that the list contains only the children who are eating today. If the child with the nut allergy is away, the child does not appear. Back tomorrow, the child appears again. The sheet on the wall cannot do that: it lists eighteen names every day, and the cook has to remember which of them apply.

A shorter list is a list that gets read to the end.

## The cook's morning

There is not just one look at the screen. There are three, and each has a different job.

The first is for quantities. Before the ingredients are weighed, the cook looks at the Total. The absences reported since the evening before, and the planned ones, are already in it.

The second is for the special plates. Shortly before cooking begins, the cook reads the allergies list and decides what will be prepared separately and with which utensils.

The third is for serving. Before the plates go out, the cook presses "Refresh", sees the final portions for each class, and reads the names again. The screen also updates by itself, but the button is there for the moment certainty is wanted.

## From the number to the tray

That the portions are per class is not a detail. In many schools the food leaves the kitchen in containers per class or per sitting. The cook does not need only "142", but "A1: 19, A2: 21". With the table in front of them, the cook divides without asking anyone.

The same holds for the special plates. The list gives the class beside the name, so the separate plate goes into the right container, with a label.

## The comments

The Comments column is for whatever is not a number: a note that concerns the class on that day. It does not replace the allergies list. It is where the kitchen sees information that would otherwise arrive with a child coming down the stairs holding a slip of paper.

## What remains a person's job

The screen shows what parents have declared. It does not know what ingredients today's recipe has and it does not check the food. The comparison between "egg allergy" and whether the dough contains egg is made by the cook.

If a parent has not written the allergy, the screen does not show it. That is why the association reminds families, at the start of the year and after holidays, to check the allergies field on their personal link.

Finally, the kitchen tablet is a work tool. The PIN is not written on it, and the screen is not left open in a place where children and visitors pass.

## Three moments in the kitchen

1. 07:00: Total 138. The cook weighs rice for 138 and not for 150 "just in case".
2. 08:10: the list has four names instead of the usual six, because two children with allergies are away. Four separate plates are prepared.
3. 11:40: "Refresh" before serving. Class D1 has dropped by one portion since the morning, and its container is filled accordingly.

## What the kitchen gains

- Portions and allergies on the same screen, from the same source
- An allergies list for only those who are eating today
- Numbers per class, ready for dividing into containers
- No phone call to find out how many plates

## How the screen enters the kitchen routine

1. Put a tablet or a phone in a fixed spot, away from water and flame.
2. Open the kitchen link and type the PIN at the start of the shift.
3. Fix the three looks: before weighing, before cooking, before serving.
4. Agree how separate plates are marked with name and class.
5. Once a term, ask parents to check the allergies they have written.
