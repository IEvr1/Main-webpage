In a restaurant with a dining room, takeaway is always the second job. The people on shift are there for the tables. The phone rings, and the nearest waiter has to choose between a customer who is looking at them and one they cannot see.

## The problem is who takes the order, not who cooks it

A taverna's kitchen can usually cope with a few bags between the dining room's plates. The ingredients are the same, the grill is already lit.

What the shift cannot cope with is the taking. A takeaway call takes three minutes from someone who has eight tables at that moment. In three minutes one table is waiting for the bill, another for its drinks, and a plate is going cold at the pass.

That is why many tavernas end up not answering the phone on Saturdays. Takeaway exists on paper and not in practice.

## What changes when the customer writes it themselves

The takeaway customer opens the shop's link. They see the menu, choose, write their notes, and set when they will call in. They give a mobile number and get a confirmation SMS.

Nobody on the floor interrupted anything. The waiter does not even know an order came in, and does not need to.

## One queue in the kitchen, two sources

The kitchen already has its own way for tables: tickets, shouts, whatever you use. The screen with the takeaway queue goes beside it, not in its place.

The cook sees the takeaway cards with the collection time on them. That time is what allows them to be fitted between tables. A bag for a quarter past nine does not need to go ahead of a table that ordered at ten to nine.

## The collection time is your tool

In a full kitchen, "as fast as possible" is the enemy. The customer who sets a time gives you room. And the earlier they order, the more.

Encourage it. One line on the page and on your profile: "On Saturdays, order takeaway in the afternoon for the time you want." Families who take food home every Saturday learn it within two weeks.

## What you offer for takeaway

The online menu need not be the dining room's whole list. The fish that needs twenty minutes of attention and the dish that does not survive a box can stay on the tables only.

A smaller takeaway menu, with dishes the kitchen turns out quickly and that travel well, protects both the dining room and your reputation. A customer who opens a box of soggy chips will not think the distance was to blame.

## When the kitchen has no more room

There are evenings when the dining room takes the whole kitchen: a wedding, a feast day, a full Saturday in August. Then the right thing is to accept no takeaway at all.

From the dashboard you declare a closure. The page stops accepting orders for that period, and the customer sees it before filling a cart. It is better than a phone that rings unanswered: the customer gets a clear "not tonight" and not silence.

If you know beforehand that an evening is booked for an event, declare the closure on the Monday.

## Collection without stopping the floor

The takeaway customer arrives and stands at the entrance, among those waiting for a table. If they have to find a waiter to ask for their bag, you have lost what you gained.

Fix a spot: the end of the bar, a shelf beside the till. The bags wait there with a name. The customer shows the SMS, takes the bag, and is gone within half a minute.

## With or without delivery

Most tavernas start with pickup only, and that is enough. If you have somebody for deliveries, the same menu supports them, with a pin on the map and a radius check. But delivery is an additional decision, not a precondition.

## Three Saturday moments

1. 20:40, the dining room full. Four takeaway orders enter the queue within a quarter of an hour, and no waiter went near the phone.
2. A family orders at 17:00 for collection at 20:00. The kitchen prepares the bag in the gap between two waves of tables.
3. An evening with a private event: the shop has declared the closure beforehand, and nobody sends an order to a kitchen that cannot take it.

## What the taverna gains

- Waiters stay with the tables
- Takeaway becomes real income on full evenings too
- The kitchen fits the bags in according to collection time
- The customer hears a clear "not tonight" when it cannot be done

## How you organise it

1. Choose which dining-room dishes go on the takeaway menu.
2. Put the queue screen where the cook sees it, beside the tickets.
3. Fix a collection spot that does not pass through the dining room.
4. Decide on which evenings you declare a closure for takeaway.
5. Tell regulars to order early with a time.
