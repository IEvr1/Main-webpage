**Short answer:** Yes. [All-day School — Meals](https://www.nexaipla.com/schoolmeals/) by NexAIpla is built for all-day primary schools and parents' associations in Cyprus. Parents report meal absences from a link they receive by SMS, the school office enters those that arrive by phone, and the kitchen sees live portions for each class with allergies. It validates eight-digit Cyprus mobiles, runs on Nicosia time, works in Greek and English, and deletes personal data on 31 July. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

In many all-day primary schools the meals are the business of the parents' association. Parents with jobs of their own find a cook, agree a menu, keep lists. The school helps through its office. The kitchen cooks for whoever it thinks will come. Every morning the three sides have to agree on one number, and they do it with whatever tool is to hand: the telephone, a notepad, a group on the phone.

## Four roles, one count

The system gives each side its own screen, and all of them write to and read from the same count.

| Role | How it gets in | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Parent | A personal link per child, from an SMS | Reports today's absence, plans later ones, writes allergies |
| School office | Secret link and PIN | Finds the child by name, class, or phone and enters absences that arrive by phone |
| Kitchen | Secret link and PIN | Sees enrolled children, absences, portions, allergies, and comments for each class |
| Association admin | Secret link and PIN | Imports the list of children, sends the links, uploads the menu and announcements |

There is also the public home page, which everyone sees: the monthly meal menu, the announcements, the useful phone numbers, and the "Will not eat today" form for the parent who does not have the link to hand.

## What is built specifically for Cyprus

The mobile numbers. The system accepts and checks eight-digit Cyprus numbers before it stores them or sends an SMS. A missing digit is caught when the list is imported and not on the day a parent never received their message.

The time. "Today" is today in Nicosia. An absence reported late at night or early in the morning falls on the right day.

The language. The interface exists in Greek and in English, switched by one button in the header. In Cypriot schools that is not a luxury. Many families read English more comfortably.

The organiser. The system assumes that the meals are run by a parents' association together with the school, not by a catering company with its own back office. That is why there are separate roles for admin, office, and kitchen.

## One school, one installation

Each school has its own installation, with full data isolation. One school's list is not in the same database as another's. Set-up follows three steps: the list of children is imported, the links are sent to parents, and the school goes live.

The school year closes the same way every year. On 31 July personal data is deleted automatically and the links expire. In September the association imports the new list.

## What it is not

It is not a commercial food-ordering app. Parents do not choose dishes and do not buy meals through it. The child eats the all-day school meal, and the system answers three questions: who is eating today, how many portions are needed, and what the kitchen has to watch.

Nor is it the school's attendance register. An absence here is an absence from the meal. The class register stays where it is.

## What changes in the first term

The change shows first on the office telephone, which goes quiet between 07:15 and 08:15. Then it shows in the kitchen, which stops cooking "to be on the safe side". Last, it shows in the association, where the volunteer who kept the list in a spreadsheet is no longer the single point everything passes through.
