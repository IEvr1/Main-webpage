**Short answer:** In [All-day School — Meals](https://www.nexaipla.com/schoolmeals/) the staff screens are protected by two things together: a secret link and a PIN for each role, that is admin, school office, and kitchen. Parents have neither a PIN nor a password. They enter through a personal, random link per child that they receive by SMS, and they see only their own child. All links expire on 31 July. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

When a parents' association hears "no password", it worries. These are children, family phone numbers, allergies. The worry is right. But the answer is not one password for everybody. It is a different lock for a different user, according to what each can see.

## Two kinds of user, two kinds of lock

A parent sees one child: their own. They can report that the child will not eat, plan absences, and write allergies. They see no other family, no list, no number. For that level of access a personal link that cannot be guessed is enough, and it loses no parent at a login screen.

Staff see many children. The office searches the whole list. The kitchen sees allergies for each class. The admin role holds everything. Here one key is not enough, which is why there are two.

## The staff's two keys

Each staff role has its own secret link and its own PIN. You open the link, see the PIN screen, type it, and press "Enter". Both are required. Someone who finds the link without the PIN does not get in. Someone who learns the PIN without the link has nowhere to type it.

After the PIN something happens that cannot be seen but matters: the link's key leaves the address bar. The session continues with a secure cookie, which scripts on the page cannot read, which is valid only for that role, and which expires with the school year. If someone glances at the office screen later, they cannot copy the link from the address.

## The parent's link

The personal link is long and random. It is not derived from the child's name or the phone number, so it cannot be guessed. When the parent asks for recovery, or the association sends it again, the link is renewed. And on 31 July it expires, like the staff links.

The parent has one duty: not to forward it. The link is a key to their child, not a message for the class group.

## Protection around the public forms

The home page has two forms that accept a mobile number: the absence report and the link recovery. These are the points a malicious person would try, so they have three protections.

A limit on attempts. Many tries from the same connection or for the same mobile are slowed down. The same applies to unlocking with a PIN.

Replies that give nothing away. If someone tries random numbers to learn which families have a child at the school, the form's reply does not tell them whether the number exists.

Number checks. Only valid eight-digit Cyprus mobiles are accepted, before they are stored or an SMS is sent.

All of this runs over a secure connection, and the PINs and the SMS keys stay on the server, not in the user's browser.

## Who sees what

| Role | Key | What it sees |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Parent | Personal link per child | Only their own child: today's meal, future absences, allergies |
| School office | Secret link and PIN | Search for children and the day's absences |
| Kitchen | Secret link and PIN | The day's portions, allergies, and comments, without phone numbers |
| Admin | Secret link and PIN | List of children, sending links, menu, announcements, data deletion |

## What the school itself must do

Technical protection does not rescue a PIN written on a sticker on the kitchen tablet. Give each PIN only to the people in that role. Do not send the link and the PIN in the same message. When the cook or the secretary changes, ask for new details for the role. And do not open the admin screen on a shared computer that is left unlocked.

## Three situations

1. A parent's SMS is forwarded to a relative by mistake. The parent asks for recovery on the school page and receives a new link.
2. Someone finds the kitchen link in an email. Without the PIN they see only the screen that asks for it.
3. A program tries a hundred numbers on the absence form. The attempts are slowed down, and the replies do not show which numbers exist.

## What the school gains

- Parents get in without a password, so all of them get in
- Staff need two keys, not one
- Each role sees only what belongs to it
- Every key expires with the school year

## How the keys are handed out

1. Decide which people hold each role and keep the list short.
2. Deliver each role's link and PIN through different channels.
3. Send the parents' personal links from the admin screen and not from a group chat.
4. Explain to parents that the link is personal and how recovery works.
5. Ask for new role details when staff change.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is access to the school meals system protected by a PIN?

In [All-day School — Meals](https://www.nexaipla.com/schoolmeals/) the admin, office, and kitchen roles enter with a secret link and a PIN. Parents enter with a personal link per child, without a PIN. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

### Why do parents have no password?

Because they see only their own child, and a password would leave many families out. The link is random, is renewed on recovery, and expires on 31 July. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

### What happens if someone tries many numbers or many PINs?

The attempts are limited, and the public forms do not disclose whether a mobile number is on the list. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

### Does the kitchen role have access to families' contact details?

No. The kitchen role shows portions, allergies, and comments. Contact details stay with the admin role. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).
