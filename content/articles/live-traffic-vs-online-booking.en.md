**Short answer:** It depends on the question your customers ask. If they ask "how busy are you now?", you need [Live Business Traffic](https://www.nexaipla.com/shoptraffic/), which shows walk-in customers a live colour-coded level, opening hours, and closures. If they ask "what time can I come?", you need Online Receptionist 24/7, which books a time by chat or QR and confirms by SMS. Some businesses need both. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

Both products exist to take a question off the phone. The mistake is to choose by trade, "I am a barber, so I need bookings", and not by how people really arrive at your door. Two barbers on the same street can need different products. One works by appointment only. The other has never kept a diary and never will.

## The test: listen to the phone for one week

Take a sheet of paper with two columns. In the first you put a stroke for every "now" question: are you busy, are you open, can I come over. In the second, a stroke for every "when" question: do you have anything on Thursday, what time is Maria free, can I move my appointment.

At the end of the week the longer column tells you what to buy first. It does not take a consultant's opinion. It takes a pen beside the phone.

## When the live level fits

When the service is short and its length cannot be predicted. When people drop in because they happened to be nearby. When you do not want to manage late arrivals and people who never came. Pharmacies, cafés, bakeries, walk-in barbershops: here a diary would be a burden, while a colour solves the problem.

## When bookings fit

When the service takes time and is tied to a particular person, room, or machine. When idle time is expensive. When the customer travels a distance and will not risk the trip without a firm time. Medical practices, physiotherapy clinics, salons that do colour, personal training, garages: here a colour is not enough, because the customer wants a promise.

## When you need both

When the work is mixed. The salon books colour and takes the quick cut without an appointment. The barbershop with two chairs keeps one for appointments and one open. The garage books a time for the service and takes whatever drives in for a tyre check.

In that case the two products have separate links, and neither knows about the other. The busy level is whatever the staff tap. If booked customers fill the shop, tap the level that a walk-in customer will really meet.

## What neither of them does

Neither is a marketplace that brings in strangers. Neither answers voice calls. Neither counts people with a camera. Both leave the phone where it is for whatever needs a person. Prices are quoted for your own set-up and are not listed here.

## Three businesses, three answers

1. A bakery with a lunchtime queue: nobody books an appointment for bread. Live traffic.
2. A dental practice: every visit needs a set time and a particular dentist. Bookings.
3. A salon with colour treatments and quick cuts: two links, "Book a time" and "See how busy we are now".

## What you gain from choosing well

- You choose by how customers arrive, not by trade
- The calls you actually receive go down
- Neither product asks the customer for an account
- You start with one and add the other later

## How to decide

1. For one week, mark every call as "now" or "when".
2. Count the two columns.
3. Ask NexAIpla for the product that matches the longer column.
4. If both columns are long, set up both and name the links plainly.
5. Repeat the count a month later.
