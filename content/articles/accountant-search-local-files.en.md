**Short answer:** By questioning the files instead of opening them one by one. With [DocsApp](https://www.nexaipla.com/docsapp/) an accountant organises the files by client and year, and asks in plain language: "what VAT amount was declared in the second quarter?". The answer comes with a citation to the document. The application reads PDF, Word, Excel, and scans, and runs offline on the office computer. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

An accounting office does not produce only returns. It produces paper. For every client there are returns, assessments, trial balances, contracts, letters from the tax office, rulings, leases, bank certificates. Times sixty clients. Times ten years.

## Where the time goes

The accounting software knows the numbers. It does not know the documents. When the question is "how much was the turnover", the software answers. When the question is "what did the tax office's letter say about the deadline", the answer is in a PDF.

And the PDF is called "scan_20230914.pdf".

Windows search finds file names, and sometimes words inside files that contain text. It does not find words in scans. And it does not understand questions: if you search for "deadline" and the document says "no later than", it will not find it.

So the accountant opens files. One, two, twelve. Ten minutes for a question the client considers simple.

## What changes when you question the folder

The client's files go into their folder inside the application. You ask as you would ask an assistant who has read them all:

"By when did we have to reply to the tax office's September letter?"

The application finds the related excerpts, even in different wording, and composes the answer. Below it you see which file it came from. You open it, confirm, and answer the client while they are still on the line.

## Questions from the office's daily life

About returns: "What amount of tax resulted from last year's return?"

About the client's contracts: "How much is the monthly rent and when is it adjusted?"

About correspondence with authorities: "Which supporting documents did the department ask for?"

About the client's staff: "What is the date of hire in the employment contract?"

About Excel exports: "Show expenses by category for the third quarter." From Excel and CSV files the application also produces charts.

And a one-click summary, for the twelve-page ruling you have to explain to the client in two sentences.

## The organisation that suits an accounting office

Folders go up to two levels. For an accounting office, the natural division is client and financial year.

When you ask inside "Georgiou Ltd / 2024", the answer cannot come from 2023 or from another client. That is critical in a profession where all clients have documents of the same type, with the same words and different numbers.

When you ask at client level, you cut across the years: "When did the company's registered office change?"

## The scans

Most of an accountant's archive is scans. With the optional character recognition for Greek and English, their text becomes searchable, locally.

For amounts and dates from scans, the rule is strict: you always open the original. One misread digit is a mistake that is not forgiven in this work.

## Why it matters that it is offline

The accountant holds the most personal financial details of their clients. Income, assets, debts, employees' salaries. The relationship rests on discretion.

An online AI tool would mean that those details go up to a third party's servers. Here the model runs on the office computer through Ollama. The files do not leave. When the client asks, the answer is one sentence.

## What it is not

It is not accounting software. It makes no entries, calculates no tax, files no returns, and does not connect to the tax authority's systems.

It is not a tax adviser. It answers from the documents you gave it. It does not know the legislation unless you load it, and it does not interpret. If you ask "is this expense deductible?", the right answer comes from you.

And it is not your archive. The application cleans up data automatically after six months. The originals are kept in your own system, for as many years as the law lays down, and are loaded again when needed.

## When it pays off most

In the periods when the phone rings most: before deadlines, after assessments, during audits. There, every client question answered in one minute instead of ten is time that returns to the work.

And when a colleague is away. The file that only they "knew" can be questioned by whoever sits at the computer.

1. A client asks when the shop's lease expires. The accountant questions his folder, opens the citation, and answers before hanging up.

2. Make a folder per client and a subfolder per year.
