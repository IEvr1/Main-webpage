**Short answer:** Yes. [All-day School — Meals](https://www.nexaipla.com/schoolmeals/) has a Greek/English language switch on the parents' page. Buttons, forms, and instructions appear in the language the parent chooses, so that they can report an absence, plan days, and write allergies without a translator. The monthly menu, the announcements, and the allergy text appear as they were written by whoever entered them. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

In many primary schools in Cyprus one class has children from five or six countries. Their parents work, pay taxes, and send the children to the all-day school, but the sheet that comes home in the bag is in Greek. Some photograph it and run it through a translator. Some ask the child. Some leave it.

## What is lost when there is only one language

The absence is not reported. The parent did not know they should, or did not know how. The kitchen cooks the portion and throws it away.

The allergy is not written down. That is the most serious. A parent who does not understand the enrolment form may leave blank the field that matters most.

And somebody else becomes the translator: a teacher, a neighbour, very often the child. An eight-year-old should not be the one explaining to their mother what the school writes about their food.

## What the switch changes

On the school page there is an EL/EN switch. One tap changes the language of the parent's whole environment: the section headings, the button for today's absence, the form with the mobile number, future absences with the "From" and "To" fields, the allergies field, the recovery of the personal link.

The parent does not need to understand Greek to do any of the actions that fall to them. And since there is no account and nothing to install, there are no sign-up screens to translate either.

## What does not translate itself

Honesty is needed here, because the switch is not a translator of content.

| Item | Changes with the switch? | What the school does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Buttons, forms, instructions | Yes | Nothing |
| Monthly meal menu | No, it is a photo or a scan | Uploads a menu written in both languages, if it wishes |
| Announcements | No, they are the association's text | Writes the important ones in both languages |
| Allergy text | No, it is the parent's words | Asks for plain, short words |

The menu is an image. Whatever the scanned sheet says is what the parent sees. If the school wants everyone to read it, it prepares the menu with the dishes in both languages before uploading.

Announcements are written by the association. Two lines of Greek and two lines of English in the same announcement are enough.

## Allergies in two languages

The allergies field is filled in by the parent in their own words, and the cook reads it as written. If the parent writes "peanuts" and the cook reads only Greek, the information exists but may not get through.

This is solved by agreement. Ask parents to write the food in one or two words and not a whole story. Give the kitchen a short list of the common allergens in both languages. And when an entry is not clear, the office phones the family before the first day of meals, not after.

## How families find out

The switch does not help if the parent does not know it is there. In the first message of the year write one line in English: that the page has English and where to tap. Say it at the parents' meeting too, showing it on a phone.

For families who read neither Greek nor English, the page is an ordinary web page and their phone's browser can translate it. That is not perfect, but it is better than a sheet in a bag. And the office is always available: it enters the absence for whoever phones, in whatever language they manage to understand each other.

## The school's staff

The office, kitchen, and admin screens are used by the school's own people. The bilingual experience concerns parents first, because that is where the obstacle lies.

## Three families

1. A father from the Philippines taps EN, sees the button for today's absence in English, and reports by himself for the first time, without asking his daughter.
2. A mother from Britain writes "egg" in the allergies field. The cook finds it on the kitchen's two-language list and prepares a separate plate.
3. The association posts an announcement about a change of dish with two lines of Greek and two of English. Nobody phones to ask what it says.

## What the school gains

- Parents who do not read Greek report by themselves
- More allergies get written down, because the field is understood
- Children stop translating for their parents
- The kitchen has a more accurate number from every family

## How you set it up properly

1. Write in the first SMS or the first announcement that the page is also available in English.
2. Prepare the monthly menu with the dishes in both languages before scanning it.
3. Write important announcements in two languages, in the same text.
4. Give the kitchen a list of allergens in Greek and English.
5. Decide who phones the family when an allergy is not clear.
