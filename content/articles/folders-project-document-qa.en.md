**Short answer:** With the folders in [DocsApp](https://www.nexaipla.com/docsapp/). You organise documents into folders up to two levels deep, for example client and matter, and choose in which one the question will be asked. The application searches only there, so the answer cannot rest on another client's document. Conversations are saved as threads in several tabs, so that you can work in parallel. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

A tool that questions documents becomes dangerous the moment it has many documents. With ten files, every question finds the right one. With two thousand files from sixty clients, the question "what is the amount of the advance?" has sixty possible answers, and all are true for somebody.

## Why the mixing happens

The application finds the excerpts that most resemble your question. It does not know which client you have in mind. If two contracts have an almost identical term on the advance, both are equally "relevant".

The answer you get will be faithful to a document. Just not necessarily to yours.

## The solution is an old one: folders

The application lets you organise documents into folders and confine the question to one of them. When you ask inside the "Papadopoulos" folder, the search covers only Papadopoulos's documents. The rest might as well not exist.

This is not cleverness on the model's part. It is a fence. And fences are more reliable than cleverness.

## Two levels, no more

Folders go up to two levels deep. A folder and, inside it, subfolders.

That looks like a restriction and is an advantage. Structures with six levels always end in the same place: nobody remembers where anything went. Two levels force you to decide what the basic division of your work is.

## How you choose the structure

The question is: in what way do you ask? The first level should be what you have in mind when you open the application.

An accounting office: client and, inside, financial year. "Ioannou Ltd" and inside "2024", "2025".

A law office: client and matter. "Constantinou" and inside "Shop lease", "Employment dispute".

An engineering office: project and phase. "Strovolos apartment block" and inside "Permit", "Construction", "Handover".

A small business: area and type. "Suppliers" and inside "Contracts", "Price lists".

Choose one logic and keep to it everywhere. Inconsistency is what ruins structures, not depth.

## When you ask at the upper level and when at the lower

The question in the subfolder is the most exact. "What VAT amount was declared?" inside "2024" cannot bring an answer from 2023.

The question in the client's folder is for what cuts across matters. "In which of the client's contracts is there an exclusivity clause?"

When you are not sure where something is, start wide and narrow down.

## Threads and tabs

Conversations are saved as threads. The analysis you did on Tuesday for one client is still there on Friday, with its questions and answers.

You can have several threads open in tabs. That suits the way an office really works: you are in the middle of one matter, the phone rings about another, you open a second tab, answer, and go back without losing the thread.

A good rule: one thread per matter, inside its folder. Then the thread becomes the matter's logbook.

## The separation protects confidentiality too

Confining by folder is not only a question of accuracy. When you have the client opposite you and ask something on screen, the answer must not quote an excerpt from another client's folder.

If you ask inside their own folder, that cannot happen.

It is worth remembering that folders organise the search and not the access. Whoever sits at the computer sees all the folders. Controlling who sits there is a matter for the Windows account.

## The citation as a second check

Even with folders, look at the citation. It shows which document the answer came from. If you see a file name you did not expect, something went into the wrong folder.

That is why file names containing the client or the project help. The citation "Contract_Ioannou_2024.pdf" is confirmed at a glance. The citation "scan0042.pdf" is not.

## Cleanup and order

Whatever has stayed in the application for more than six months is cleaned up automatically. For matters that have closed this is useful: old folders that blur the picture do not pile up. For those that continue, you keep the originals in your archive and load them again.

## Three ways of organising

1. An accountant with sixty clients: a folder per client, a subfolder per year. The question about 2024 VAT never brings another client's figures.
2. A lawyer opens three tabs for three matters running on the same day, each with its own thread.
3. An engineer asks at project level which phase the structural study refers to and then narrows to the permit subfolder.

## What you gain

- Answers come only from the client or project you are asking about
- Two levels the whole office remembers
- One thread per matter, which serves as a history
- Parallel work in several tabs

## How you set up the folders

1. Decide what the basic division is: client, project, or area.
2. Decide the second level and write the rule in one line.
3. Make three folders first and try them with real questions.
4. Give files names that contain the client or the project.
5. Open one thread per matter and name it accordingly.

In [DocsApp](https://www.nexaipla.com/docsapp/) you choose the client's folder before asking, and the search covers only that folder's documents. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).
