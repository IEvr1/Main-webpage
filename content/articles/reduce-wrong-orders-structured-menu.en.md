> **Quick answer:** By removing the person who listens and writes. In [Online Food Orders](https://www.nexaipla.com/foodorder/) the customer selects dishes and quantities from a structured menu, writes the notes in their own words and, for delivery, places a pin on the map instead of dictating an address. They receive an SMS with what they ordered and can correct it. The kitchen reads exactly the same order. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://nexaipla.com) (nexaipla.com).

A wrong order is rarely down to carelessness. It is down to the information changing hands three times in noise. Voice became writing, writing became a shout towards the grill, the shout became a plate. Something is lost at each handover.

## Where mistakes are born

In the ear. The extractor is running, the line crackles, the customer is talking from a car. "Without" and "with" are one syllable apart.

In the hand. The employee writes abbreviations only they understand. "2 CH NO" was clear when it was written.

In the memory. "Oh, and some chips" at the end of the call, when the employee has already turned towards the till.

In the address. Numbers that sound alike, streets with the same name, a floor that was never mentioned.

And in assumption. "The usual" from a customer whom today's employee is seeing for the first time.

## What one mistake costs

The plate that is made again is the smallest cost. Add the driver's second trip, which delays the next two deliveries. The ten minutes on the phone with an angry person. The soft drink you give away to calm them.

And what cannot be measured: the customer who never complained and simply ordered from elsewhere next time.

## What a "structured" menu means

It means the customer does not describe what they want. They choose it. Every dish is a specific item on the list, with a name and a price. The quantity is a number the customer taps, not a word that is heard.

A whole family of mistakes disappears that way. There is no "I thought you said chicken". The customer tapped pork, the card says pork.

## The notes in the customer's words

Whatever does not fit the menu goes in the cart notes. The difference from the phone is who writes it. The customer types "well done, no tomato, sauce on the side", and exactly that sentence appears in the kitchen.

If there is a misunderstanding, it is in writing and it is clear who wrote what. That also ends the "I told you so" argument that nobody ever wins.

## The pin instead of the address

For delivery the customer places their spot on Google Maps. The system checks whether it is inside your radius, and the driver receives the same spot. The "40 instead of 14" mistake cannot happen, because nobody said and nobody heard a number.

## The second check is done by the customer

After sending, the customer has an SMS with a link to their order. They see it written down. If they forgot something or tapped wrongly, they correct it themselves before it is prepared.

On the phone the customer never sees what the employee noted. Here they see it before the heat does.

## How you set up a menu that does not confuse

The tool helps, but you write the menu. A few rules cut mistakes further still.

Names that stand apart. If you have "Mixed grill 1" and "Mixed grill 2", the customer will tap the wrong one. "Mixed grill for two" and "Mixed grill for four" are not confused.

Sizes as separate items. Small and large pizza are two lines with two prices, not a note.

A description that says what the dish contains. When the customer reads that the pitta has onion, they write "no onion" before ordering and not after biting into it.

And in both languages. A customer who guesses what a word means will order the wrong dish with perfect accuracy.

## Which mistakes remain

The system does not cook. A plate can still come out with tomato because the cook did not read the note, or a bag can leave without the soft drink. Those are mistakes of execution and are solved at the pass: whoever closes the bag reads the card line by line.

The difference is that there is now something correct to compare the bag against.

## Three mistakes that no longer happen

1. "Two chicken, one pork" written down the wrong way round on the phone. Now the customer taps the quantities personally.
2. An address in the wrong community. Now the pin lands on the house and is checked against the radius.
3. A forgotten "no nuts". Now it is written on the card, in the customer's words.

## What the shop gains

- Fewer plates made a second time
- Fewer double trips for the driver
- A written order on which customer and kitchen agree
- The customer corrects their own mistakes before they are cooked

## How you apply it

1. For one week, note every wrong order and where it started.
2. Rewrite the names of dishes that resemble each other.
3. Make sizes separate items with their own price.
4. Add to each dish one line with the main ingredients.
5. Lay down that the bag is closed only after it is compared with the card.
