How can European businesses use AI while complying with GDPR?

GDPR does not ban AI. It asks you to treat **personal data** lawfully: know **why** you process it, **minimise** what you collect, choose **processors** with contracts when data leaves your control, and give people **rights** (access, erasure, etc.). For most SMEs, compliance starts with **one workflow** and clear rules — not a wall of bans staff ignore.

> **Quick answer:** Map **what personal data** your AI use touches, pick a **lawful basis** (often contract or legitimate interest — confirm with your adviser), use **DPA/contracts** when a vendor processes data, prefer **local/offline** tools for confidential files when upload is not justified, and document **who can access what**. ## Where GDPR meets everyday AI use

| Scenario | Typical GDPR question |
|----------|----------------------|
| Pasting client text into a public chatbot | Are you disclosing personal data to a **processor** without a contract? |
| AI answering customer emails | What data is logged, for how long, and who can see it? |
| Document Q&A on payroll PDFs | Can files stay **on-premise** instead of cloud upload? |
| Automated booking with names/phones | Is data limited to what booking needs? |

## Practical checklist for SMEs

1. **Inventory** — List tools staff already use (including “unofficial” chatbots).
2. **Purpose** — One sentence per tool: why personal data is processed.
3. **Lawful basis** — Record the basis your adviser confirms (contract, consent, legitimate interest, etc.).
4. **Processors** — For cloud AI, check sub-processors, region, retention, training use of data.
5. **Minimisation** — Anonymise or redact where possible before upload.
6. **Staff policy** — Short “do / don’t” (see below).
7. **Rights & incidents** — Know how to handle access/erasure requests and breaches.

## Cloud AI vs local document AI

## A three-line office policy that staff keep

1. No pasting **client or staff personal data** into public AI unless approved.
2. Approved sensitive work uses the **designated local tool** on a controlled PC.
3. Check **citations** before sending AI output to customers.

## When automation is still worth it

GDPR-ready automation focuses on **low-risk, high-volume** tasks first: FAQs without personal data, booking flows with documented retention, structured forms with clear purpose. ## Frequently asked questions

### Does GDPR forbid ChatGPT at work?

Not automatically. Risk depends on **what data** you send and **which vendor terms** apply. Many offices ban unchecked paste for client data.

### Do I need a DPA with every AI vendor?

When the vendor processes personal data **on your behalf**, a processing agreement is standard. Local/on-premise tools may reduce processor count — not all obligations.
