**Short answer:** Nowhere, unless you decide otherwise. In the [AI Readiness Score](https://www.nexaipla.com/ai-score/) the scoring is done inside your browser: the 18 answers are turned into a score on your own device. Neither signup nor email is needed to see the result. The answers are sent only if you submit a contact message, which is prefilled with your score and your weakest areas. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

Most of us have learned to be suspicious of "free tests" on the internet. You answer ten questions, and at the end a field appears: "Enter your email to see the result". From that moment you receive newsletters for ever.

## What "in the browser" means

When you open the page, your browser downloads the questions together with the method of calculation. From there on the work is done on your device.

You tap an answer, and the page keeps it locally. You tap the eighteenth, and the page adds the points, converts them to a scale of 100, and draws the result.

It is like a calculator. You key in numbers and see the sum, and nobody else learns what you added.

## What is sent, and when

While you answer and while you look at the result, your answers are not transmitted.

There is only one point at which that changes, and you control it: the contact form. If you wish to write to us about the result, the message is prefilled with your score and the areas that came out weakest.

You see it before you send it. If you do not press send, it does not go.

## No signup

You create no account. You give no email to begin, nor to see the score.

The result appears straight after the last question: the number, the colour, the score by dimension, and the likely inefficiencies worth fixing first.

## Why it was designed this way

For a reason that has nothing to do with politeness. A readiness test is useful only when answered honestly.

If you believe someone is reading, you will answer a little more flatteringly. You will say the processes are "for the most part" written down. Out will come a 68 that is not true, and you will make decisions on it.

When you know the number is for your eyes only, you have nobody to impress.

## What this means in practice

We do not receive your answers or your score, unless you send them to us. So we cannot remind you of the result, nor tell you next month what you had scored.

The consequence is simple: keep it yourself. Note the number, the date, and the lowest dimension, or take a screenshot. Do not count on finding it there when you reopen the page.

When you want to compare in six months, you will need that note.

## On a shared computer

If you take the test on the till computer or on a device others use as well, close the tab when you finish. The result is on the screen, and whoever sits down next will see it.

It is the same precaution you would take with any document of the business.

## When it is worth sending the result

When you want a second opinion on what it means. The prefilled message spares you explaining from the beginning: it already says where you stand and what came out weak.

When the lowest dimension is something you do not know where to start with.

When you are already considering a particular application and want to know whether your score supports it.

And when none of those holds, you do not send. The test has done its job.

## What to do privately with the result

Discuss it inside the business. Ask your partner or an experienced employee to take it separately, and compare.

Choose one change in the lowest dimension and make it.

Repeat later. How the number is read is explained in [what the 0–100 score means](/en/articles/ai-readiness-score-0-100-meaning).

## A question worth asking of every such tool

Before you answer any online test about your business, look at two things. Does it ask for contact details before showing the result? And does it say plainly what happens to the answers?

If it asks for an email to "unlock" the score, your answers are the price. Here the result appears without your giving anything.

## Less data, fewer worries

There is also a simple principle behind all this: what is not collected cannot be lost, leaked, or used otherwise.

It is the same principle we would suggest for your own business when you design a tool: use only what is needed.

## Three uses

1. An owner takes the test in the evening, sees 41, notes the number, and closes the tab. Nobody learns that he took it.
2. Two partners take it separately on their phones and compare screenshots the next day.
3. An office manager sees a low score in data and sends the prefilled message to ask where to start.
