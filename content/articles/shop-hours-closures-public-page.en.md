**Short answer:** A shop publishes its opening hours, closed days, and sudden closures on its public page in [Live Business Traffic](https://www.nexaipla.com/shoptraffic/). The page shows the weekly hours, special closed days, a clear "open" or "temporarily closed" status, and an announcement, beside the live busy level. The owner changes them from a private dashboard, which also has a one-tap emergency close. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

"Are you open?" is the oldest question a shop receives, and it ought to be the easiest. In practice the answer lives in four places that disagree with each other: a sticker on the door, a Google profile last corrected two summers ago, a pinned post, and the owner's head. The disagreement shows on exactly the days when customers most need to know: at holidays, in summer hours, on the afternoon the plumber is coming.

## Three kinds of "closed"

A shop is closed in three different ways, and customers need to tell them apart.

The first is the weekly rhythm: Wednesday afternoon, Sunday, the midday break. It never changes and it belongs in a weekly table.

The second is the planned exception: a public holiday, the week in August, the day of a family wedding. It is known in advance and belongs in the list of special closed days, so that the page already knows on the morning that day arrives.

The third is the surprise: a power cut, an illness, a delivery that blocks the entrance for twenty minutes. Nobody plans it and there is no time for editing. For this the owner dashboard has the emergency close. One tap, and the public page says the shop is temporarily closed. One more tap reopens it.

## What the customer reads

At the top of the page the customer sees whether the shop is open now. Not the hours from which to work it out, but the answer. Under it are the weekly hours for anyone planning tomorrow, and the announcement line for whatever needs words: "Summer hours until 31 August", "Closed on Friday for stocktaking", "Open on the bank holiday, 09:00–13:00".

Because the busy level sits on the same page, the status and the level never contradict each other. A shop that is temporarily closed does not show LOW and invite people to come.

## One place that is always right

The page does not replace your Google profile. It gives you one address that can be corrected in seconds from a phone, and that everything else can point to. Put the link in the Google profile, in the social bio, and as a QR on the door itself. The door QR matters most on the day the door is locked: whoever is standing in front of it learns in ten seconds whether to wait five minutes or come back tomorrow.

Then keep one simple discipline. Whenever the hours change, change them on the page first. The other places follow when there is time.

## Three days when the hours were not the usual ones

1. A bakery stays closed on 26 December. The day was entered at the start of the month, and that morning the page already shows the shop closed, with the announcement "Back on the 27th".
2. A barber wakes up ill. From bed he taps the emergency close and writes "Closed today, back to normal tomorrow". The three regulars who check the page do not make the trip.
3. A café loses power at noon. The owner closes on the page and reopens with one tap forty minutes later, when the lights come back.

## What changes

- "Open now" or "temporarily closed" is stated outright and not left to be worked out
- Closed days are entered once, ahead of time
- A sudden closure is published in one tap, from anywhere
- Hours and busy level never contradict each other

## Announcements that help

An announcement works when it is short, dated, and removed on time. "Closed on Monday 6 January" is useful until the 6th and confusing on the 7th. For a day with shorter hours, write the times in the announcement in figures. Make a habit of reading your own page every Monday morning the way a customer would. If the line has gone stale, delete it. An empty announcement line is better than an old one.

## Set-up order

1. Enter the weekly hours exactly as they are, including the midday break.
2. Add every known closed day for the next three months.
3. Try the emergency close once on a quiet morning, so that you know where it is.
4. Put the public link on Google, on social profiles, and as a QR on the door.
5. Check the page every Monday and clear old announcements.
