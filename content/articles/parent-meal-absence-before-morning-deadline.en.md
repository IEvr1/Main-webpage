**Short answer:** [All-day School — Meals](https://www.nexaipla.com/schoolmeals/) does not lock the report at any hour: a parent reports from their link whenever they find out, and the kitchen's number changes at once. The hour by which a report is in time for the cooking is decided by the school together with the kitchen and announced to parents. For absences known in advance there are future absences with dates. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

The question "by what time" has two answers, and most misunderstandings arise because they are confused. The first is technical: until when the system accepts the report. The second is practical: until when the report changes how much food goes into the pot.

## What the system does and does not do

The system has no clock that closes the form. A parent can press "will not eat today" at six in the morning, at eight, or at ten. Each time the absence is recorded and the kitchen table takes one portion off the child's class.

That is deliberate. A report that comes late is still useful: the cook knows that one plate will be left over, the office knows that the child is away, and the day's count stays true. If the form closed, the parent who was five minutes late would not report at all.

What the system does not do is decide on your behalf at what hour the food is "committed". Only your kitchen knows that.

## How the school finds its own hour

Ask the cook one thing: what is the last moment at which a quantity can still change. In a kitchen that cooks on site, that is usually the moment the ingredients are weighed. In a school that receives prepared food, it is the hour the number is given to whoever prepares it, which may be much earlier.

The hour announced to parents should be a little before that moment and easy to remember. "By 08:00" is remembered. "By 07:40" is not.

## Where the hour is written

On the school's home page, as an announcement that stays active all year: "Report a meal absence by 08:00 so that the portion is not cooked." In the message that goes with the personal links in September. And in what the office says, every time somebody phones.

One hour, the same everywhere. If the announcement says eight and the office says half past eight, parents will keep the more convenient one.

## Absences known since yesterday

Most absences are not a surprise. The dentist's appointment was made two weeks ago. The trip has been known since last month. The class outing is in the calendar.

For these, the personal link has "Future absences": the parent sets a "From" and a "To" date and is done. On the morning itself there is nothing to remember, and the kitchen has had the number since the day before.

The more parents use it, the less the morning hour matters. It is worth saying it outright: "If you know in advance, report in advance."

## When the parent finds out late

The child wakes with a fever at half past seven, and by the time the family decides what to do it is a quarter past eight. The instruction to the parent is simple: report it anyway. The portion may already have been counted, but the record is right and the kitchen knows one plate will be left over.

If the school also wants to be told by voice about late absences, it asks for that: "After 08:00, report online and phone the office." The office sees the day's entries on its own screen and can let the cook know.

## The mistaken report

The opposite also happens: the parent reported an absence and the child went to school after all. From the same link they turn the day's switch back. If they do not manage to, the office removes the absence from the list of the day's entries, and the portion returns to the count.

## Three mornings

1. 06:50: a father reports from bed that his daughter has a virus. Class C2 drops from 22 to 21 portions before the kitchen is lit.
2. Sunday evening: a mother sets a future absence from Wednesday to Friday for a trip. For three mornings she has nothing to remember.
3. 08:25, after the school's hour: a parent reports and phones. The cook keeps the portion for second helpings.

## What one clear hour changes

- Parents know until when a report saves a portion
- The cook knows at what moment the number is steady enough to weigh out
- Planned absences leave the morning rush
- Late reports are recorded instead of being lost

## How you set the hour

1. Ask the kitchen what the last moment is at which a quantity changes.
2. Choose a round hour a little earlier.
3. Write it as a permanent announcement on the school page.
4. Explain future absences to parents for anything known in advance.
5. Agree what a parent does on finding out after the hour.

## Frequently asked questions

### By what time must a meal absence be reported?

[All-day School — Meals](https://www.nexaipla.com/schoolmeals/) accepts the report at any hour. The hour that is in time for the cooking is set and announced by each school. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

### Does the form close after a certain hour?

No. There is no automatic lock. A late report is recorded as usual and changes the number the kitchen sees. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).
