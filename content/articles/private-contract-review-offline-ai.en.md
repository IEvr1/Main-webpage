**Short answer:** Yes. With [DocsApp](https://www.nexaipla.com/docsapp/) the contract goes into the intake folder on your computer and does not leave it. You ask for a one-click summary and put questions about term, termination, penalty clauses, or payments. Every answer cites the document for checking. The tool helps you read faster and does not replace legal advice. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

Contracts are the kind of document AI seems made for: long, dense, with the important part buried on page thirty. They are also the kind of document that must not be uploaded anywhere. Many themselves contain a term forbidding their disclosure.

## Why the online chatbot is not an option

When you paste a contract into an AI service on the internet, you disclose it to a third party. That may breach the contract's own confidentiality clause. If you are a lawyer or a consultant, it may also breach professional secrecy.

And the other party will never find out, until the day they do.

## The contract that does not leave

The application runs on your computer, with models executed locally through Ollama. You drop the file into the intake folder. The text is read, indexed, and questioned on the same machine.

There is no third party to whom anything is disclosed. You can check by disconnecting the network: the application goes on answering.

## First reading: the summary

With one click you get a summary of the document. It is not for deciding. It is for getting your bearings: who the parties are, what the subject is, how long it lasts, where the money is.

After the summary you know what to ask.

## Second reading: the questions

The questions worth asking of every contract are almost always the same.

Term and renewal. "How long does the contract last and does it renew automatically?"

Termination. "How can each party terminate, and with how much notice?"

Payments. "What are the payment terms and what is provided for delay?"

Penalty clauses and liability. "Are there penalty clauses or a limit of liability?"

Exclusivity and non-competition. "Is there an exclusivity commitment or a ban on competition?"

Price adjustment. "Can the price change and in what way?"

Law and jurisdiction. "Which law applies and which courts are competent?"

For each answer you see the citation. You open the text and read the term itself. The answer tells you where to look. What holds is the term.

## The question about what is missing

Some of a contract's most serious problems are what it does not say. No ceiling on liability is provided. What happens in a case of force majeure is not defined.

Ask explicitly. When the application replies that it found no relevant provision in the document, that is a finding and not a failure. It shows you what to raise in the negotiation or with your lawyer.

## Comparing versions and related documents

Put in the same folder the draft you received, the previous contract with the same party, and the annexes. Ask what the previous one provided on termination and what the new one provides.

Because the application answers only within the folder you choose, it will not mix in terms from another client's contract.

## Scanned contracts

Signed contracts are usually scans. With the optional character recognition for Greek and English they become text locally.

Here double care is needed. One misread digit in an amount or a deadline changes the meaning. For every number that counts, you open the original.

## The limits, stated plainly

The tool reads. It does not judge. It does not know whether a term is unfair under your country's law, whether a clause is usual in the sector, or whether a price is reasonable.

It can also miss something. A term worded in an unusual way, or an exception in an annex, may not be retrieved. That is why the reading with the tool is the first reading, not the last.

For a lawyer, that means locating faster the points they will study personally. For a business owner, it means arriving at the lawyer's with specific questions and not with "have a look".

## After signing

The contract does not end when it is signed. Six months later you will need to recall when the deadline for non-renewal expires. If the file is in the client's folder, the question is answered in a few seconds.

Note that the application cleans up data automatically after six months. You keep signed contracts in your normal archive and load them again when you need them.

## How you review a contract

1. Make a folder for the matter and drop the contract and annexes there.
2. Ask for a summary to get your bearings.
3. Ask the seven basic questions and open each citation.
4. Ask explicitly about whatever you would expect to be there and did not see.
5. Give the findings to your legal adviser before you sign.
