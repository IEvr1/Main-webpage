Most conversations about AI assume everything happens somewhere on the internet. For many small businesses that is fine. For some it is not, and you need not be a bank to be one of them.

## Three words that get confused

Internet service. The tool runs on some provider's computers. You open it from a browser. The data travels there to be processed.

On-premise. The tool runs on your own machine, inside your premises. It may have a connection for updates or so that several office computers can see it, but processing is done locally.

Offline. The same, with no connection whatever. The machine can have its cable pulled out and the tool goes on working.

## Who really needs it

Lawyers, with case files and confidentiality.

Accountants, with the financial records of dozens of clients.

Medical offices and laboratories, with health information.

Businesses that have signed confidentiality clauses with their own clients.

Those who hold something that is the product itself: recipes, wholesale price lists, designs.

And, more prosaically, those in an area where the connection drops twice a week.

## Who does not need it

A tool that answers "what time do you close?" works with information that is already on your door. There is nothing to protect.

If the task's data is public or of no consequence, a local installation adds cost without benefit. An honest adviser will tell you so.

## What you gain

The data stays in the building. You need not trust anyone's promises about what happens to it, because it goes nowhere.

Work does not stop when the internet goes down.

You do not depend on whether an outside service will be available tomorrow or will change its terms.

And the answer to the client who asks "where do my details go?" becomes one sentence.

## What you pay

A machine. Local models want a computer with plenty of memory. An old office machine is often not enough.

Capability. The models that fit on an office computer are smaller than the largest ones running in data centres. For many jobs that does not matter. For some it does.

Responsibility. Backups and updates become your affair. If everything is on one computer and its disk fails, nobody else has a copy.

Access. The tool is where the machine is. You do not open it from your phone on the beach.

## What local models do well

Search and questions over your own documents.

Summaries of lengthy texts.

Extracting particular fields from standardised documents, such as dates, amounts, and names.

Sorting documents by kind.

First drafts of texts based on your own templates.

## And what less well

Tasks that need very broad general knowledge or recent information from the internet. A model with no connection does not know what changed last week.

Very complex reasoning over many steps.

The picture changes fast, and each year more can be done locally. That is why the answer for your own task should be today's.

## The mixed solution

You need not choose one of the two for the whole business.

The part that touches client files runs locally. The part that answers general questions or sends reminders works on the internet. Each task goes where it fits.

## A ready-made example

[DocsApp](https://www.nexaipla.com/docsapp/) is an application of this kind that already exists. It installs on Windows, runs the models locally through Ollama, and answers questions over your documents with no connection.

If your need is questions over documents, you most likely do not need a bespoke project. If it is something else with the same confidentiality requirement, the same approach is applied to a bespoke tool.

## What to write in the description

What kind of data the task touches, and why it must not go out.

What computers you have. You need not know the technical specifications. The make, model, and year of purchase are enough to begin with.

How many people will use it, and whether they all sit in the same place.

How reliable your connection is.

With those, the feasibility percentage and the cost range in the reply will concern the local solution, and not a general one.

## The answer may be "you do not need it"

If the description shows that the data is not sensitive, the recommendation will be the simplest solution. A local installation is a tool for a particular problem, not a badge of quality.

## Three cases

1. A law office wants summaries of case files. It chooses a fully offline solution on one office computer.
2. An accountant wants amounts extracted from clients' vouchers. Processing is done on-premise, and the rest of the office sees it over the internal network.
3. A guesthouse on an island with an unstable connection wants a tool that works when the internet drops as well.

## What the local solution offers

- The data does not leave your premises
- The task carries on without internet
- No dependence on an outside service being available
- A simple answer to clients about where their details are

## How you start

1. Decide which task really touches sensitive data.
2. Note which computers you have and how old they are.
3. Describe the task and ask explicitly for a local solution.
4. Read in the reply what a local model does well and not so well for your job.
5. Organise backups before you put in real data.
