> **Quick answer:** Yes. In [Online Receptionist 24/7](https://www.nexaipla.com/onlinebooking/) the customer signs up nowhere. They open the booking page from a link or a QR code, choose service, person, and time through a short chat, and give a mobile number. They get a confirmation SMS with a link, from which they cancel or change the appointment. There is no account, password, or app. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://nexaipla.com) (nexaipla.com).

Booking systems were designed by people who love accounts. Profiles, history, loyalty points. A barber's customer does not want a profile. They want to know whether you have a gap at six.

## What the sign-up screen asks

It asks someone who came for one thing to do five. Give an email. Think of a password. Open their mail. Tap a confirmation link. Go back and log in.

At every step some of the people leave. They do not cancel anything. They simply never reach your calendar. Some message you on social media. Some phone. Some go elsewhere.

And those who do sign up will have forgotten the password by the next appointment, six weeks later.

## What an appointment really needs

Four things. Which service. With whom. When. And a way for you to reach the customer.

The fourth is the mobile number. It is what they would give you on the phone too. None of these requires a password.

## How the booking is made

The customer opens the page. They find it from a link on your profile, or by scanning a QR code in the window, at the till, on your card.

Booking happens through a short chat. They choose a service. They choose a person, if you have more than one. They see the free times and tap one.

The times they see really are free. Availability is calculated live, by service and by person.

They give their mobile number. Done.

## The SMS in place of the account

The customer gets a confirmation SMS. Inside is a link, personal to that particular appointment.

The link does what an account would do. It shows the appointment. It allows cancelling. It allows changing the time. Without a login, because the link is already the proof that this is the same person: it was sent to their mobile.

Before the appointment an automatic reminder comes too, with the same link.

## "And how do I know who it is?"

You know as much as you always did. You have a mobile number that receives messages. On the phone you had a voice and a name you may have heard correctly.

The number is in fact more reliable than an email. People have five addresses and one mobile.

## "And fake bookings?"

The worry is reasonable. With no sign-up, will not anyone book whatever they like?

In practice, sign-up never stopped the person who wanted to be a nuisance. The one it stopped was the ordinary customer. The booking is tied to a mobile that receives the message, and your board shows every appointment. If something looks odd, you see it and phone.

The real problem for small businesses is not fake bookings. It is real customers who forget to come. For them there are the reminders and the easy cancellation.

## What you store

Less. There are no customer passwords, so there are no passwords to leak. You do not collect email addresses you will not use. You keep what the appointment needs.

And you get into the appointment board with a signed link, without a password. The same logic applies on both sides.

## Where it fits

In any business where the appointment is simple and recurring. Hair salons and barbers. Beauty institutes and spas. Medical offices and clinics for a first visit or a follow-up. Gyms for personal training. Repair shops for the service appointment.

It is not a marketplace with many businesses where the customer compares you with a competitor. It is your own page, with your own services.

And it does not answer phone calls. Booking is done in writing, by the customer.

## The customer who does not get on with technology

They benefit most. They would never have made an account. But they know how to scan a code or tap a link their daughter sent, and they know how to read a message.

For those who want not even that, your phone is always there. The aim is not to abolish it. It is for it to ring less.

## Three bookings

1. A customer walks past the barber's at nine in the evening, scans the QR code in the window, and books for Saturday in one minute.
2. A customer aged sixty-eight taps the link her daughter sent, chooses a colour with her own hairdresser, and gets an SMS.
3. A patient changes the time of a follow-up from the SMS link, without remembering any password.
