**Short answer:** By putting the menu on an ordering page such as [Online Food Orders](https://www.nexaipla.com/foodorder/). The customer picks dishes from a bilingual menu, writes notes, chooses pickup or delivery, and gives only a mobile number, with no account. They receive a confirmation SMS with a manage link, and the order appears in the kitchen's live queue without anyone typing it in. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

The phone is the worst employee in the kitchen. It turns up only when there is work, interrupts whoever it finds, wants quiet in a room with an extractor fan, and keeps no notes. Even so, in most small restaurants it is still the main door for takeaway orders.

## What one phone call costs

A phone order lasts two to four minutes. The customer asks what the mixed grill includes, changes their mind about the pitta, asks whether the shop delivers to their neighbourhood, and dictates an address that the employee writes down with one hand.

On an evening with forty orders, that is about two hours of a person holding a handset instead of a spatula. And while that person is talking to one customer, the line is engaged for the rest.

There is also the cost that does not show. The "no onion" that was heard as "with onion". The "14 St Nicholas Street" that was written as "40". The order that was told to someone who never passed it on to the grill.

## What the ordering page does in its place

The customer opens the shop's link on their phone. They see the menu with categories and prices, in Greek or in English. They put dishes in the cart and write their notes in their own words.

They choose pickup or delivery. For delivery, they place their spot on the map and learn at once whether it is inside the area you serve. They give their mobile number and send.

A few seconds later they have a confirmation SMS with a link. From there they see their order, change it, or cancel it. You picked up nothing.

## What the kitchen sees

The order appears in the dashboard's live queue, in writing: dishes, quantities, notes, how it will be handed over, the time. There is no handwriting to decipher and no voice to pick out from the noise.

The dashboard opens through a secure link on a tablet or on the owner's phone. There is no password for the whole staff to share, and no till with special software.

## The phone does not disappear

Some customers will go on phoning, and there is no reason to turn them away. The aim is to move most orders, so that the phone rings five times an evening instead of forty.

That happens when the link is everywhere the number is today: on the shop's map profile, on social media, on the pizza box, on the slip that goes into the bag. A QR code at the till catches those who came to collect and will order again next week.

You can also record a short voicemail greeting: "For faster service, order from the link you will find on our page."

## What it is not

It is not a platform with many restaurants where your customer also sees your competitors. It is your own page, with your own menu, on your own deployment with your own data.

Nor is it a voice assistant that answers the phone. The idea is that the phone is needed less, not that a program talks into it.

## How quickly it is set up

If your menu is ready, with categories, dishes, and prices, the setup takes less than an hour. Most of that time goes on deciding what you will offer online and how far you will deliver.

## Three evenings

1. A souvlaki shop with 45 orders on Saturday: 32 come from the page. The person who used to hold the phone wraps pittas.
2. A pizzeria in a tourist area: visitors who do not speak Greek order from the English menu instead of hanging up awkwardly.
3. A café with breakfasts: nearby office workers send an order at 08:40 for collection at 09:00, without waiting on the line.

## What the shop gains

- The line is never engaged, because there is no line
- Every order arrives in writing, with the customer's notes
- One person returns from the handset to production
- The customer changes or cancels by themselves from the SMS

## How you begin

1. Prepare the menu with categories, dishes, and prices in both languages.
2. Decide whether you will offer pickup, delivery, or both, and set the delivery area.
3. Set up a tablet in the kitchen with the order dashboard open.
4. Put the link and the QR code wherever you print the phone number today.
5. For two weeks, count how many orders came from the page and how many by phone.
