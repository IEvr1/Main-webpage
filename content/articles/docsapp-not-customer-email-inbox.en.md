**Short answer:** No. [DocsApp](https://www.nexaipla.com/docsapp/) is a document assistant that runs offline on Windows. It does not connect to an email account, does not read incoming mail, and does not send replies to customers. The word "inbox" in the application means a folder on your disk where you drop files for indexing. For email overload there are other solutions, through [Custom AI Tools](https://www.nexaipla.com/ai/). Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

The misunderstanding is understandable. The application has a folder called inbox, and in English the word also means incoming mail. Add that half the internet advertises "AI that answers your email", and the conclusion draws itself. It is wrong, though, and better cleared up before you install anything.

## What the "intake folder" is

It is an ordinary Windows folder. You open it with File Explorer, like Documents or Downloads. You drag a PDF there, a Word file, an Excel file.

The application watches that folder. Whatever it finds, it reads, splits into pieces, and puts in the index, so that you can question it.

That is all. There is no mail address, no connection to an email provider, no sending.

## What the application does

It answers questions about documents you have given it. With a citation to the source, and with a statement when the answer is not in the documents.

It makes summaries in one click.

It produces charts and analysis from Excel and CSV files.

It organises documents into folders and answers only within the folder you choose.

It keeps your conversations as threads.

And it does all this without the internet, with models that run on your computer through Ollama.

## What it does not do

It does not connect to Gmail, Outlook, or any other account.

It does not read your incoming mail and does not sort it.

It does not draft and does not send replies to customers.

It is not a chatbot for your website.

It does not book appointments and does not take orders.

The reason is not technical weakness. It is a design choice. A tool whose value is that it does not talk to the internet cannot at the same time send email.

## Why the distinction matters

If your problem is "I cannot keep up with replying to customers' messages", this tool will not solve it, and you will be disappointed.

If your problem is "I have three hundred documents and cannot find what each says, and I cannot upload them to a cloud", that is exactly what it solves.

They are two different jobs. The first is communication: something comes in from outside and something has to go out. The second is knowledge: something you already have must become accessible.

## Can I question an email if I save it as a file?

Yes, and here lies the only real contact between the two worlds. If you save an important exchange as a PDF or a text file and drop it into the intake folder, it becomes a document like any other.

That is useful for correspondence with evidential value: an exchange with a client about the terms of an agreement, a letter from a public authority, a notice from a bank. You keep it in the matter's folder and question it together with the contract.

You do that yourself, file by file. The application does not pull messages from anywhere on its own.

## They can coexist

Yes. An accounting office can use the document assistant for client files and, separately, a solution for communication. The two exchange no data, and that is the point: confidential documents stay on the computer, even if correspondence passes through services on the internet.

## Three cases

1. A lawyer expects the application to sort his incoming mail. He learns that it does not, and uses it for case files, which were his bigger problem.
2. An accountant saves as PDF the correspondence with the tax office about a client and questions it together with his returns.
3. A salon owner with a hundred messages a day about appointments is pointed to online booking, not to the document assistant.
