**Short answer:** The kitchen knows from the live portions screen in [All-day School — Meals](https://www.nexaipla.com/schoolmeals/). For each class it shows the children enrolled, today's absences, and the portions to prepare, with the total at the bottom. The numbers change as parents and the school office register absences, so the cook reads a count instead of collecting messages. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

Ask a school cook how many children will eat today, and the honest answer at 08:00 is often "about the same as yesterday". The real figure is split between a note on the office desk, two phone calls nobody wrote down, and a message in a chat the cook is not a member of. So the kitchen cooks for everyone and hopes, or cooks for fewer and hopes harder.

## What the screen shows

One row for each class. In each row: Enrolled, Absences, Portions, and beside them Allergies and Comments. At the bottom, the Total for the whole all-day school. Portions are simply the enrolled children minus today's absences, and the sum is done by the system and not in somebody's head.

There is a "Refresh" button, and the table also updates by itself during the day. A cook who looks at 08:00 and again at 09:30 sees the same table with newer numbers.

## Where the numbers come from

Three sources feed the same count. Parents who report from the school page with their mobile number. Parents who use the child's personal link, for today or for later dates. And the school office, which registers the absences that still arrive by phone. The kitchen does not need to know which came from where. Every absence, whoever entered it, takes one portion off the right class.

Because future absences are already in the system, the morning figure does not start from nothing at 07:00. The trip reported last week and the long illness have already been counted.

## By class, not only a total

The total tells you how much to cook. The breakdown by class tells you how to serve it. If lunch goes to the classrooms or is served in shifts, the staff know that Class 3 has nineteen today and not twenty-two, before anyone walks down the corridor to count heads. A wrong number is also easy to spot this way: a class with ten absences on an ordinary Tuesday is a question worth putting to the office.

## Access for the kitchen only

The kitchen opens its screen with its own secret link and a PIN. It is a separate role. The cook sees the day's portions, allergies, and comments, and neither sees nor manages the list of families, the parents' phone numbers, or the settings. If the kitchen tablet is left on the counter, what is on it is today's count and not the school's records.

## What changes in the cooking

The gain is not dramatic on any single day. It is three portions here, five there. Over a school year it is the difference between a kitchen that throws food away out of habit and one that cooks what will be eaten. The mood at 08:00 changes too. The cook no longer waits for someone to bring a number, and no longer walks to the office to ask for it.

## Three days in the kitchen

1. At 07:50 the table shows 142 portions. At 08:40, after six more absences, it shows 136 and the cook corrects the rice before it goes on.
2. Year 5 leaves on a trip at 07:30. The office finds the class by search and enters its children as absent, and the row drops to zero portions before cooking begins.
3. A flu week: the cook watches the total fall three mornings in a row and orders less bread for Thursday.

## What the kitchen gains

- A real number before cooking begins
- A breakdown by class for serving
- Less food cooked for children who are not there
- No walk to the office and no group chat to do the counting

## A morning in the kitchen

1. Open the kitchen link on the tablet and enter the PIN.
2. Read the total and the rows at the hour the kitchen has agreed with the school.
3. Press "Refresh" just before the quantities are fixed.
4. Check allergies and comments for the children who are eating today.
5. If a class looks wrong, ask the office before cooking and not after.
