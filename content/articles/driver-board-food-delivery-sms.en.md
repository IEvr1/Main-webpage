> **Quick answer:** In [Online Food Orders](https://www.nexaipla.com/foodorder/) drivers have their own board, with a separate signed link. Each delivery passes through three stages: ready, on the way, done. When the driver taps "on the way", the customer automatically receives an SMS with an estimated arrival time. The shop sees where each delivery stands, and nobody phones to ask. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://nexaipla.com) (nexaipla.com).

"Where is my order?" is the call nobody wants. The customer makes it annoyed. The employee does not know the answer. They phone the driver, who pulls over to reply, and so the delivery is later still.

## The gap between the pass and the door

Inside the kitchen the order is visible: it sits in the queue, somebody is working on it. At the customer's door it is visible too: the bell rings. In between lie ten to twenty minutes when nobody sees it.

Most complaints are born in that interval, even when nothing went wrong. A customer who waits twenty minutes with no news gets angrier than one who waits thirty and knows the food is coming.

## The driver's board

The driver opens their own link on their phone. They see only what concerns them: the orders that are for delivery, with the drop-off point and the customer's notes.

They do not see the shop's figures or the settings. They cannot change the menu or declare a closure. Their link is different from the owner's, and that is deliberate: in many shops drivers change often, and you do not want the owner's link sitting on phones you do not control.

## Three taps

Ready. The kitchen has finished and the bag is waiting at the pass. The driver knows it can be collected.

On the way. The driver has taken the bag and is setting off. With that tap an SMS goes to the customer with the estimated arrival time. It is the message that replaces the phone call.

Done. The bag has been handed over. The order closes and the driver sees the next one.

Three taps per delivery, with a thumb, without typing. The driver writes nothing and phones nobody.

## What the customer receives

One SMS at the moment that matters. It is not an app notification that requires an installation. It reaches any mobile, including that of the grandfather who ordered with his granddaughter's help.

With the estimated time the customer knows when to go down to the entrance, when to lay the table, when to call the children. From the manage link received with the confirmation they can also look at the status of the order whenever they like.

## What the shop sees

The owner sees which orders are waiting at the pass, which are on the road, and which have closed. If a bag stays "ready" for ten minutes, they see it before it goes cold. If a delivery has been "on the way" for a long time, they know which one without asking.

When a customer phones regardless, the employee has an answer: "It left six minutes ago."

## With one driver or with five

With one driver, the board is their list: what to take now, what follows. With more, they all see the same ready orders, and you need to agree a simple rule for who takes which, for example by neighbourhood or in turn.

The system does not follow the driver's position on the map. It is not a surveillance tool. It records three events the driver declares, and those are enough for the customer to be informed.

## The habit that has to be built

The whole benefit hangs on one tap: "on the way" before the engine starts, not at the third traffic light. In the first days, remind them. After a week drivers do it by themselves, because they see that customers are waiting at the door instead of taking their time to come down.

## Three deliveries

1. The driver taps "on the way" at 20:52. The customer gets an SMS with an arrival time and comes down to the entrance exactly as he pulls up.
2. Two bags stay "ready" for eight minutes. The owner sees it and sends the second driver.
3. A customer phones out of habit. The employee looks at the screen and answers in five seconds, without disturbing the driver.

## What changes

- Fewer "where is my order" calls
- The driver does not answer the phone while driving
- The customer waits at the door and the handover takes less time
- The owner's link is not shared with drivers

## How you start

1. Give drivers the link to the driver board, not the owner's.
2. Show the three stages with a test delivery on your own phone.
3. Agree that "on the way" is tapped before the vehicle moves.
4. Set a rule for who takes which order when there are several drivers.
5. After a week, ask the till how many tracking calls it received.
