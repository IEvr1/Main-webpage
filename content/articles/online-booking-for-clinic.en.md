A medical office is not a hair salon, but the appointment problem is the same and indeed sharper. Patients all phone at the same hour. Those who cannot get through ring again, and the queue grows. The person standing at the desk waits for the call to end.

## What a medical office needs from an appointment system

Visits of different length. A first visit is not a follow-up, and a minor procedure is not a prescription renewal.

A schedule per doctor. Each has their days, their operating sessions, their on-call duties.

A way to close the schedule when the doctor is away at a conference.

A way to move appointments when something urgent comes up.

And fewer missed visits, because an empty hour in a practice with a three-week wait is a waste for everybody.

## How the patient books

From the link on the practice's page or from a QR code in the waiting room. They choose the visit type and the doctor, see the times that are actually free, and select.

They fill in a mobile number. They make no account and remember no password. For older patients, and for their children who book on their behalf, that matters.

They get a confirmation SMS, and a reminder before the visit. If they cannot come, they cancel or change the time from the link in the message.

## Visit types and durations

Most of the setup work is done here. You define the types you offer and how long each lasts: first visit, follow-up, check, session.

Availability is calculated on those durations. A forty-minute first visit will not be offered in a twenty-minute gap.

A piece of advice: name the types neutrally. "First visit" and "Follow-up", not names of conditions. The system needs to know how much time to hold, not why.

## Per doctor

In a practice with several doctors or therapists, the patient chooses whom to see. One's availability does not affect another's.

Each doctor sees their visits in their Google Calendar. In the morning, before reaching the practice, they know what the day looks like.

## Conferences, leave, operating days

They are declared as closures on the board. The patient sees no times on those days, and the secretary need not remember not to give them out.

## When the doctor has to leave

An urgent case at the hospital, and the practice's afternoon is cancelled. The board has an emergency reschedule for exactly that case. The appointments are moved from there, instead of the secretary phoning twelve patients one by one.

## What it is not

This part must be absolutely clear.

It is not a medical record. It keeps no history, diagnoses, or test results. Those belong in the system you already use for that purpose.

It does not do prescriptions, telemedicine, or billing.

It does not answer phone calls.

It does not judge whether a case is urgent. A patient with an acute problem should not be hunting for a free time on a page. Write it plainly where you give the link: for urgent cases, phone or go to the hospital on duty.

And it is not a directory of doctors where the patient compares practices. It is the page of your own practice.

## Privacy

An appointment at a medical office is in itself sensitive information, and deserves care.

Booking needs little: visit type, doctor, time, mobile number. Keep it there. Do not use the appointment system for clinical notes.

Access to the board is by signed link, with no shared passwords. Give it only to those who need to see the schedule.

As a practice you have your own obligations concerning patient data. If you have specific requirements, tell us before setup, so that we can discuss them on the basis of how you work.

## The front desk afterwards

The secretary does not become redundant. Her day changes. Fewer calls of the kind "have you anything next week?", more time for the patient standing in front of her.

Whoever phones is served as always. The secretary books the visit from the same page, and the patient gets a reminder like everyone else.

## Which practices benefit most

Those with scheduled visits of steady length: dentists, physiotherapists, dermatologists, ophthalmologists, dietitians, psychologists, paediatricians for routine checks.

Those that work mainly with unscheduled urgent cases benefit less.
