> **Quick answer:** The score in the [AI Readiness Score](https://www.nexaipla.com/ai-score/) is the sum of your answers to 18 questions, converted to a scale from 0 to 100. Each answer gives from 0 to 3 points, and the questions are divided among six dimensions. The number shows how ready the business is today to benefit from AI. It does not grade the business itself, and the breakdown by dimension says more than the total. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://nexaipla.com) (nexaipla.com).

A number with no explanation is noise. 52 may be good news for a business that had never thought about the subject, and bad for one about to spend on tools next month. To read it correctly, you need to know where it comes from.

## How the number is made

Eighteen questions. In each you choose one answer, worth from 0 to 3 points.

The maximum is 54. Your sum is converted proportionally to a scale of 100.

If you collected 27 points, that is half, the score is 50. If you collected 36, it is about 67.

That means something practical too: each point you gain on a question raises the score by a little under two. A single answer that improves from 1 to 3 is worth almost four.

## The six dimensions

The questions are not shuffled. They belong in threes to six areas, and each can give up to 9 points.

Strategy and use cases. Do you know which job you want AI for, or only that "we ought to do something"?

Data readiness. Are your records somewhere a tool can read them, or in notebooks and heads?

People and skills. Is there someone who will use it and look after it?

Process and operations. Is the work done the same way every time, or however it turns out?

Tech and integration. Can the programs you have talk to each other?

Governance and change. Who decides, who is responsible, and how does the team take to changes?

## What the total says

The total places you in one of three coloured bands. We explain them in detail in [what red, amber, and green mean](/en/articles/red-amber-green-readiness-bands).

In a few words: low down, the base is still missing. In the middle, some pieces exist and others do not. High up, the ground is ready and the question becomes "where do we start".

## Why the breakdown counts for more

Two businesses on 55 may have nothing in common.

The first has middling marks everywhere. Nothing is wholly missing, nothing is strong.

The second has nearly full marks in four dimensions and nearly zero in data. It knows what it wants, has the people, has the tools, and its records are on paper.

The first needs general improvement. The second needs one thing, and once it has done it will be far higher. The total does not tell them apart. The score by dimension tells them apart at once.

That is why the result shows both, along with the likely inefficiencies worth fixing first.

## What the score does not mean

It is not a grade for your business. A shop with loyal customers and healthy profits can score 30, because everything is done from memory and by hand. It is a good business that is not yet ready for automation. Those are two different things.

It is not a forecast. An 80 does not guarantee that a project will succeed. It says the preconditions exist.

It is not an audit by a third party. It rests on what you yourself answered.

## Honesty is the only ingredient

Because it is a self-assessment, the number is as accurate as the answers.

Answer for how things are today, not for how you plan them to be. "We are going to organise our files" is not organised files.

When you hesitate between two answers, choose the lower. A slightly strict score protects you. A slightly generous one sends you to buy something that will not work.

You have no reason to flatter. The scoring is done in your browser, and nobody sees the answers unless you send them.

## How the number is used

As a starting point. Note the score and the date. In three or six months, after you have made some changes, repeat.

As an occasion for discussion. If you are two partners, answer separately and compare. The questions on which you disagree are often more illuminating than the score itself. If one believes the processes are written down and the other does not, you have just found something.

As a guide to priority. The lowest dimension is usually the first thing that deserves attention, before any purchase.

## Small moves, a visible difference

Because the questions are few, no transformation is needed to move the number.

Naming one person responsible. Writing one process on one page. Putting the customer list in one spreadsheet instead of three notebooks. Each of those changes one answer, and the next measurement will show it.

The point of course is not for the number to rise. It is that the moves that raise it are the same ones that make the business easier to run, with or without AI.

For the wider frame, see [what AI readiness is](/en/articles/what-is-ai-readiness).
