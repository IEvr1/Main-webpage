**Short answer:** With three messages that go out by themselves. In [Online Receptionist 24/7](https://www.nexaipla.com/onlinebooking/) the customer gets a confirmation SMS as soon as they book, and an automatic reminder before the appointment. Both contain the same link, from which they cancel or change the time by themselves. The time that is freed shows as available at once, and your board updates without a single phone call. An appointment is not one moment. It is a small story that lasts days. The customer books. A week passes. They forget or remember. Something comes up or does not. They come or do not. At each point in that story, someone has to talk to someone.

## The first message: the confirmation

It goes out the moment the customer completes the booking. It says what the receptionist would say on the phone: which service, with whom, what day, what time.

It does two jobs. The first is certainty: the customer knows the appointment exists, and does not need to phone to ask "did you put me down?".

The second is memory. The message stays on their phone. When in five days they wonder "was it Tuesday or Wednesday?", the answer is in their messages and not on your phone.

## The second message: the reminder

It goes out automatically before the appointment. You do not need to remember it.

Most people who do not turn up are not rude. They are absent-minded. They booked ten days ago, wrote it down nowhere, and the day came without their noticing. The reminder catches exactly those.

It catches a second group too: those who already knew they would not come, but had not found the moment or the nerve to phone.

## The link: cancelling and changing

Both messages contain the same link. It is personal to that particular appointment.

From there the customer does two things without talking to anyone. They cancel. Or they change the time, seeing the ones that are really free and picking one.

They log in nowhere and remember no password. Tap, choose, done.

## Why easy cancellation is good for you

It sounds backwards. Why make easy the thing you do not want to happen?

Because cancellation is not your enemy. The enemy is the empty chair you find out about when it is too late. The customer who cannot come will not come, whether you make it easy or not. The question is whether you learn it a day before or ten minutes after.

A cancellation phone call is awkward. People put it off. A tap is not.

## What happens to the time that was freed

It returns to availability at once. The next customer who opens the page sees it free and can book it.

You did nothing. You crossed nothing out of the notebook, you notified nobody. The appointment board shows the new picture, and if you sync with Google Calendar, it changes there too.

## What you see

The board with the day's and the week's appointments, as they are at this moment. The key figures. And the tools for your own side: declaring when you are closed, and rescheduling appointments in an emergency, when someone on the team falls ill.

You get in with a signed link, without a password.

## Why SMS

An SMS wants nothing from the customer. No app, no account, no notifications they may have switched off. It reaches every phone, old or new.

And it gets read. Emails are lost among offers. An SMS is seen by the recipient within a few minutes.

## What it does not do

It does not phone the customer and does not answer calls. All communication is written.

It does not persuade someone who has decided not to come. It reduces forgotten and unannounced absences. It does not bring them to zero.

And it does not replace your relationship. The customer who wants to talk to you always can.

## What to measure

Before you start, count three numbers for two weeks: how many appointments you had, how many people did not come without notice, and how many phone calls you took about changes.

Count again a month later. The difference in the second number is income. The difference in the third is time.

## Three appointment stories

1. A customer books for Friday. On Thursday she gets a reminder, remembers she has a doctor's visit, and changes to Saturday from the link. Friday is booked by someone else within an hour.
2. A patient cancels at eleven at night, an hour at which he would never phone. The practice sees it in the morning, with the time already free.
3. A customer wonders what time he had booked. He opens the SMS instead of phoning the barber.

## What the business gains

- Fewer customers forget their appointment
- Cancellations come earlier and in writing
- The free time becomes available again with no action from you
- Fewer interruptions while you are working

## How you put it into operation

1. For two weeks, count absences and change calls.
2. Book a test appointment and read the messages as the customer does.
3. Try cancelling and changing the time from the link.
4. Tell customers that they can change an appointment from the SMS.
5. Count again after a month and compare.
