**Short answer:** The three colours of the [AI Readiness Score](https://www.nexaipla.com/ai-score/) are bands of the total score. Red, from 0 to 39: "Not ready yet". Amber, from 40 to 69: "Partially ready". Green, from 70 to 100: "AI-ready". The colour does not judge the business. It shows what kind of step makes sense now: building the base, closing particular gaps, or choosing the first application. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

We all know how to read a traffic light. Red says stop, green says go. In the readiness score the reading is slightly different, because no colour says "do nothing". Each says "do this kind of thing".

## Red: Not ready yet

A score from 0 to 39.

The picture behind a red result is usually familiar. The business's information is in notebooks, in messages, and in the memory of two people. The same job is done differently depending on who is on shift. Nobody has been made responsible for how the tools work. And the idea about AI is general: "we ought to do something".

A great many healthy small businesses are here. It is no disgrace, and it is no verdict.

What makes sense: not to buy anything called AI yet. A tool that rests on records which are not written down has nothing to work with.

Instead, three foundation moves. Write one process, the one repeated most, on one page. Gather one kind of record, the customer list for example, in a single place. Name one person who will be responsible.

None of the three costs money, and all improve the business regardless of AI.

## Amber: Partially ready

A score from 40 to 69.

It is the widest band and the most interesting, because it hides very different businesses. Some pieces exist. Some are missing. The question is which.

Here the overall colour helps less than the score by dimension. Look at where you are strong and where not.

What makes sense: one narrow, specific use, which stands on your strong dimensions and does not depend on the weak ones. If your processes are clear but your data scattered, choose something that automates a process without needing history.

Alongside, work on the lowest dimension. The result shows you the likely inefficiencies worth fixing first.

Ready-made applications for one particular job often suit this band. They solve one problem without asking the business to be ready in everything.

## Green: AI-ready

A score from 70 to 100.

The base exists. You know what you want, your records are accessible, there are people to run it, the processes are steady, the tools connect, and somebody decides.

The question changes. It is no longer "can we?". It is "what first?".

What makes sense: to choose by value. Which task eats the most time or causes the most mistakes? Start there, with a small piece that can be measured.

Green is not a guarantee. It says the ground is good, not that whatever you plant will take. A badly chosen project fails in a ready business too.

## At the edges of the bands

39 is red and 40 amber. The difference between them may be a single answer.

The bands are a guide, not a cliff. A business on 41 is far more like one on 38 than one on 68. If you are close to a boundary, read the advice for the neighbouring band as well.

The same holds at 69 and 70. If you are just below green, see which one answer keeps you there.

## A good colour can hide a hole

A total of 72 can come from five strong dimensions and one nearly empty.

If the empty one is governance, meaning it has not been decided who is responsible and how you manage data, the green is misleading. A project will start easily and stumble at the first question "who approves this?".

So, before you read the colour, look at the six separate scores. How they come out is explained in [what the 0–100 score means](/en/articles/ai-readiness-score-0-100-meaning).

## Three common patterns

Ideas without fuel. High strategy, low data. You know exactly what you want, and the records needed are on paper. First job: digitising one particular set.

Tools nobody uses. High technology, low people. You have bought programs and the team still works with the old spreadsheet. First job: find out why.

Order without a steering wheel. Strong processes, weak governance. Everything runs smoothly, but nobody has the authority to change anything. First job: settle who decides.

## How one changes band

Some dimensions move fast. A process is written in an afternoon. A customer list is moved to a spreadsheet in a week. A responsible person is named in one meeting.

Others take time. Skills and the team's attitude to change do not shift by decision.

Start with the fast ones. Getting from red to amber is usually a matter of two or three specific moves.

## What not to do

Do not chase green by answering generously. Nobody sees the result, so the only person you fool is yourself.

Do not wait for green before doing anything. An amber business can gain a great deal from one well-chosen application.

And do not take red to mean "AI is not for us". It means "not yet", and "yet" is the important word.

## Three colours, three moves

1. A garage comes out red, 31. The owner writes on one page how a car is received, and moves the customers from the notebook to a spreadsheet.
2. A beauty institute comes out amber, 58, with strong processes and weak data. It starts with online bookings, which need no history.
3. An accounting office comes out green, 76. It picks the task that costs it the most hours and asks for a feasibility opinion on that.

## What the colour tells you

- What kind of step makes sense today
- Whether it is worth buying something or preparing first
- How close you are to the next band
- That no band means "do nothing"

## How you act according to the band

1. Look at the colour and then at the six separate scores.
2. In red, write one process and gather one set of records.
3. In amber, choose one narrow use that stands on your strong points.
4. In green, pick the task with the greatest value and start small.
5. Repeat the test after three to six months.
