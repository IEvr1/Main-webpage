> **Quick answer:** With a signed link. In [Online Food Orders](https://www.nexaipla.com/foodorder/) the owner receives a secure link that opens the dashboard directly: live queue, key figures, closures, bulk cancel, and settings. There is no username and no password to be forgotten or shared. Drivers have a different link, which opens only the delivery board. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://nexaipla.com) (nexaipla.com).

In a restaurant the password is never secret. The shift changes, the tablet logs out, somebody has to get in now, and the password is called out across the counter. In six months twelve people know it, five of whom no longer work there.

## What a signed link is

It is an address that contains, along with where it leads, a digital signature. The signature is produced by the server with a secret key that never leaves it. When you open the link, the server checks that the signature is genuine and lets you through.

In practice that means two things. The link cannot be guessed, because it is not a word or a date of birth but a long string of characters. And it cannot be forged, because whoever changes even one character gets a signature that does not match.

For you it is a bookmark. You tap it and see the orders.

## Why not a password

A password has three problems that a small shop has no time to solve.

It gets forgotten. And when it is forgotten at nine on a Saturday night, orders keep arriving on a screen that will not open.

An easy one gets chosen. The shop's name and the year. That is the first thing anyone wanting to get in will try.

It gets shared. One password for the whole staff means you do not know who has it.

The link does not solve everything, but it removes the first two entirely. There is nothing for you to remember and nothing for anybody to guess.

## Two links for two jobs

The owner's link opens the whole dashboard. There you find the order queue, the day's figures, the shop closures, the bulk cancel for emergencies, and the settings, such as VAT and your country's time zone.

The drivers' link opens only the delivery board. The driver sees what is ready, declares that they have set off and that they have delivered. They see no figures, change no settings, cancel no orders.

That separation is the most useful part of the design. Drivers are the people who change most often and who work with their personal phones. Their link can do few things, so even if it ends up in the wrong hands, the damage is small.

## Who holds the owner's link

The fewer the better. The owner on their phone. The kitchen tablet, saved as a bookmark. Perhaps the shift leader.

Treat it like the key to the shop. You do not tape it to the door and you do not hand it to whoever walks by. Specifically:

Do not send it to staff group chats. Do not print it. Do not open it on a shared computer where it will stay in the history. And lock the tablet's screen when the shop closes.

## If the link leaks

It happens: a phone is lost, an employee leaves on bad terms, a message is forwarded by mistake. Then you ask for a new link to be issued, so that the old one stops working. Write to us at once and do not wait to see whether something happens.

The same goes for the drivers' link when staff change.

## And the customers

The same logic applies on the other side. The customer has no account. They get an SMS with a link to their own order and from there they change or cancel it. Nowhere in the system is there a table of customer passwords, because there are no customer passwords.

## The data behind the link

Each restaurant runs on its own deployment. Your link opens only your shop, and your data is isolated from every other business. There is no shared platform where a mistake in permissions would show your orders to somebody else.

## Three situations

1. Saturday night, the tablet restarts. The shift leader taps the bookmark and the queue is back in five seconds, without hunting for a password.
2. A driver leaves. The shop asks for a new drivers' link. The owner's link is not affected.
3. The owner checks the figures from a phone at home, without remembering a username.

## What the owner gains

- No password to forget at the peak
- No easy password to be guessed
- Drivers have access only to deliveries
- No staff or customer passwords are stored

## How you handle it properly

1. Save the owner link as a bookmark on your phone and on the kitchen tablet.
2. Give drivers only their own link.
3. Write down which people hold each link.
4. Lock the tablet's screen at closing time.
5. Ask for a new link when staff change or a device is lost.
