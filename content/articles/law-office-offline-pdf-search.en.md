A lawyer's work is to a great extent reading. And reading has a familiar problem: the passage you need is never where you remember it. Was it in the third supplementary pleading or in the reply? Was the date in the letter or in the formal notice?

## Why online tools are ruled out

For a lawyer the matter is not one of preference. Professional secrecy covers whatever the client entrusts. A case file uploaded to an AI service on the internet is disclosed to a third party, often in another country, on terms nobody in the office has read.

The risk is not theoretical. It is disciplinary, and it is a matter of trust: no client wants to learn that their case went through a chatbot.

## The alternative that stays in the office

The application is installed on a Windows computer. The language models run on it through Ollama. The documents are indexed on its disk. The chat opens in the browser, at the address of the machine itself.

You can unplug the network cable and go on working. That is also the argument you will give, if you are ever asked.

## What you ask a case file

Facts and dates. "When was the claim served?" "What date does the formal notice bear?"

Allegations. "What does the other side allege about delivery of the goods?"

Evidence. "In which documents is invoice 1423 referred to?"

Amounts. "What amount is claimed as damages and how is it broken down?"

Testimony. "What did the witness say about the March meeting?"

A one-click summary, for the twenty-page ruling or the expert's report.

With every answer you see the source. You open the document and read the passage itself. The answer is a pointer. In your pleading you quote the original.

## The chronological order of events

One of the most useful uses is the chronology. You ask in succession: what happened before the contract, what followed, when the first demand was sent. Each answer with its citation.

Because the conversation is saved as a thread, the chronology is built up gradually and stays there for the next time you open the case.

## Organisation by client and matter

Folders go up to two levels: client and matter. The question is asked inside the matter, and the search does not go outside it.

In a law office that is not a convenience; it is a necessity. Two tenancy disputes have almost identical documents. An answer that takes the date from the wrong case is worse than no answer.

With several tabs, you work on the day's matters in parallel, each with its own thread.

## Scanned pleadings

Whatever comes from the court or from the other side is usually paper that was scanned. With the optional character recognition for Greek and English, the text is read locally.

Stamps, handwritten notes, and poor photocopies are read worse. For dates of service and deadlines, confirmation in the original is not optional.

## What it does not do, and this must be said

It is not a case-law database. It does not know rulings or statutes you have not loaded. If you give it the rulings you have gathered on an issue, it will question them. It will not find others.

It does not write a legal opinion. It summarises what the documents say. Whether an allegation holds is your judgement.

It can omit things. If a fact is mentioned in unusual wording or on a badly scanned page, it may not be retrieved. When the application replies that it did not find something, that means "I did not find it" and not "it does not exist". Before telling the court that something does not appear from the file, you check it personally.

And it is not the office archive. Data in the application is cleaned up automatically after six months. Case folders are kept as always, and are loaded again when the case comes back to life.

## Which computer, which user

Whoever sits at the computer sees every matter that has been loaded. In an office with associates who must not see one another's cases, that determines where the application is installed.

The computer needs a password, a screen lock, and an encrypted disk. Those apply to any machine with case files, with or without the tool.

## When you gain the most time

On the eve of the hearing, when you need one specific passage.

When you take over a case from another colleague and have to learn the file in a day.

When the client phones and asks about something that happened three years ago.

And in large case files, where nobody can any longer hold them all in their head.

## Three moments in a law office

1. The eve of a hearing: the lawyer asks in which document the other side admits receipt, and finds the passage in an annex to the reply.
2. A new associate takes over a case. He asks for summaries of the main pleadings and builds a chronology with citations in one morning.
3. The application replies that it found no mention of a letter of guarantee. The lawyer checks the scans personally before asserting it.

## How a law office begins

1. Pick one current case with a large file.
2. Make a client folder and a matter subfolder and load the files.
3. Install character recognition for the scanned pleadings.
4. Ask ten questions to which you know the answer and check every citation.
5. Lay down as a rule that no passage is quoted without reading the original.
