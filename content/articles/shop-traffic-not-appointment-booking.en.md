**Short answer:** No. [Live Business Traffic](https://www.nexaipla.com/shoptraffic/) does not book appointments. It shows walk-in customers how busy a shop is right now, with four colour-coded levels, opening hours, and announcements. There are no time slots, no reservations, and no queue numbers. For appointments NexAIpla has a separate product, Online Receptionist 24/7. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

The confusion is understandable. Both products involve a link, a phone, and a customer who has not arrived yet. But they answer different questions. One answers "what time is my appointment?". The other answers "is it worth coming now?". A shop that buys the second while expecting the first will be disappointed, and so will its customers.

## What it does, precisely

- One public page per shop, in Greek and in English.
- One of four busy levels, LOW, MEDIUM, BUSY, VERY BUSY, each in its own colour.
- Weekly hours, special closed days, and an "open" or "temporarily closed" status.
- One announcement line.
- A private owner screen opened by a secure personal link, with one button per level and an emergency open or close.

## What it does not do

| What you might expect | What actually happens |
| --- | --- |
| The customer picks a time | There are no times. The customer sees a level and decides when to come. |
| The page holds a place in the queue | Nothing is held. Whoever arrives first is served first. |
| The customer gets an SMS reminder | No SMS is sent. Nobody gives a phone number. |
| The system knows who is coming | It knows nothing about individual customers. No names and no accounts. |
| The level is measured automatically | The owner or the staff tap it. |

## Why the limits are deliberate

A walk-in shop that adds reservations stops being a walk-in shop. Somebody has to honour the held place when the person is late, explain it to those who are waiting in person, and deal with the people who never show up. That is real work and it needs a real diary. The busy page leaves the walk-in promise intact: nobody jumps ahead, everybody simply has better information.

There is a second reason. A level can be wrong for ten minutes and nobody is hurt. A reservation that is wrong for ten minutes means two people for one chair.

That is also why the page is so light. With no customer data, the customer has nothing to fill in and the shop has nothing to guard beyond its own owner link.

## Which shops it fits

Shops where people drop in and the service is short: barbershops without appointments, pharmacies, cafés, bakeries, small service counters, the walk-in chair of a salon. The test is simple. If your customers ask "are you busy?", this is the product. If they ask "do you have anything on Thursday at five?", it is not.

## Fit or no fit

1. A barber who never takes bookings wants regulars to stop phoning about the queue. Fit: the live level answers them.
2. A physiotherapist wants patients to choose a 45-minute session with a named therapist. No fit: that is [Online Receptionist 24/7](https://www.nexaipla.com/onlinebooking/), with slots and SMS.
3. A salon books colour treatments and accepts walk-ins for quick cuts. Both: a booking link for the first, the busy page for the second.

## How to say it to customers

Word the link so that it cannot be misread. "See how busy we are now" is right. "Check availability" is wrong, because availability sounds like an appointment time. On the page itself, the announcement can add: "No appointment needed, first come, first served."

The staff should use the same words. When someone asks at the counter whether they can book for tomorrow, the answer is either "we do not take bookings, have a look at the page before you set off" or "yes, from the other link".

## What you gain by being clear

- You buy the product that matches the question your customers ask
- Walk-in fairness stays intact, with no held places
- The customer fills in nothing and you look after none of their data
- There is a clear path to bookings when part of the business needs them

## Choosing between the two

1. Write down the three questions you hear most often on the phone.
2. If they are about the queue and the hours, ask for [Live Business Traffic](https://www.nexaipla.com/shoptraffic/).
3. If they are about a day and a time, ask for the booking product.
4. If you hear both, set up both links and name each one plainly.
5. Tell the staff the difference in one sentence, so they can repeat it at the counter.
