A barbershop without appointments runs on a waiting bench. When the bench is full, the next person through the door looks at it, does the sum, and often leaves. When it is empty, nobody knows. The shop is the same and the barbers are the same. Only the information is missing.

## The rhythm of a walk-in chair

Most barbershops know their waves by heart: the rush before work, the lull at eleven, the after-school haircuts, and the Saturday that starts full and stays full. The regulars know them too, more or less. Newcomers do not, and even regulars guess wrong in the week before a holiday or on the eve of school photo day.

A queue you can see only by arriving punishes both sides. The customer who finds five people waiting has lost the trip. The barber who watches someone turn around at the door has lost a haircut that might have fitted at 14:00.

## One tap between two haircuts

The owner screen of [Live Business Traffic](https://www.nexaipla.com/shoptraffic/) has four buttons: LOW, MEDIUM, BUSY, VERY BUSY. It opens from a personal link, usually saved on the home screen of the phone that sits beside the mirror. The natural moment to tap is when one customer stands up and the next sits down: a glance at the bench, a colour, and the clippers are back in your hand.

With two or three chairs, agree who taps. If everyone can, nobody does. The simplest rule is that the level belongs to the chair nearest the door, or to whoever takes the money.

## The bench translated into colours

In a barbershop the levels translate naturally. LOW: a chair is free, come in. MEDIUM: one person waiting per barber. BUSY: the bench is full. VERY BUSY: people are standing, come back later. In a salon where a colour treatment holds a chair for two hours, count free chairs and not people.

Remember that customers read the level as a statement about the whole shop. If one barber is in demand and the other is free, say so in the announcement line: "Short wait for Andreas, Nikos is free now."

## Scenes from the bench

1. Saturday 09:40, three men on the bench. The barber taps BUSY. A father with two boys sees it and comes at 13:00, when the page has gone back to MEDIUM.
2. Tuesday 15:00, an empty shop. The owner taps LOW and a student who keeps the page on his home screen walks in ten minutes later.
3. In the week before Easter the salon writes "open on Monday too this week" in the announcement and keeps the level honest all day. Fewer people message to ask whether there is room.

## Walk-ins and appointments in the same shop

Many salons run both: appointments for colour and treatments, walk-ins for a quick cut or a beard trim. [Live Business Traffic](https://www.nexaipla.com/shoptraffic/) covers only the second half. It does not hold a chair and it does not give a time. For the booked half the product is [Online Receptionist 24/7](https://www.nexaipla.com/onlinebooking/), where the customer picks a service, a person, and a slot, and receives an SMS.

The two work together without confusion, provided each link says what it is. "Book a time" on one. "See how busy we are now" on the other.
