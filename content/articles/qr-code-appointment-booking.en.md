The customer scans it with the phone's camera, the booking chat opens, they choose service, person, and time, and receive a confirmation SMS. They download no app and create no account. The window is the most expensive advertising medium you have, because you pay for it with the rent. When you are open, it works: the passer-by comes in and asks. When you are closed, it shows opening hours and a phone number that will not answer.

## What the code is

Nothing complicated. It is the address of your booking page, printed in a form the camera reads.

The customer lifts the phone, points it at the code, and a notification appears. They tap it and are on your page. Modern phones do this from the camera, with no special program.

From there the process is the same as with the link. The code is simply the way to get from the physical world to the page without typing.

## Where you put it

In the window, at eye level, where it can be seen with the shutters down or the lights off. That is the position that works when you do not.

At the till. The customer who is paying is the best moment for the next appointment.

On the mirror at each station. The customer sitting forty minutes with a colour on has the phone in her hand.

On the business card and the appointment card. Where you used to write the next date in pen.

On the printed matter and leaflets you hand out in the neighbourhood.

On the vehicle, if you are a garage or a technician who drives around. Parked, it works as a second window.

On the gym's notice board, next to the timetable.

## The moment after the service

The most productive use is not the passer-by. It is the customer who has just been served.

The classic "shall I book your next one?" happens at the till, with a queue behind, with the notebook open, and the customer says "I shall ring you". With the code in front of them, they book by themselves while waiting for change, or in the car before starting the engine.

Businesses that live on repeat appointments gain more from this than from any advertisement.

## Code or link?

Both. They lead to the same page.

The link is for whatever is already on a screen: your map profile, social media, a message. There the customer taps.

The code is for whatever is on paper, glass, or a wall. There the customer cannot tap, and nobody types addresses.

## How to print it properly

Size. A code in the window must be readable from the pavement, not from ten centimetres. On a card it can be small, but not the size of a postage stamp.

Contrast. Dark on light. Codes in soft colours over a photograph look nice and do not scan.

Reflection. The window glass shines at midday. Stick it on from the inside and check it in sunlight and under street lamps.

Caption. A code with no explanation is a square. Write above it what it does: "Book an appointment now — scan". Five words are enough.

Test. Before you print fifty cards, scan the proof with two different phones.

## Common mistakes

A code that leads to the home page or a social media profile instead of to booking. Every extra tap loses customers.

A code where nobody stands. Behind the till, high on the wall, on the door that opens inwards.

A code copied from a screenshot that blurred in printing.

Many codes together, one for each social network. The customer does not know which to choose and chooses none.

## Those who do not scan

Not everyone will. Some customers are not used to it, some have an old phone, some simply prefer to ask.

For them nothing changes. You book for them from the same page, with their mobile number, and they get the SMS like the rest. The code is one more door, not the only one.

## What the code does not do

It is not a membership card or a ticket. It is not scanned on arrival.

It is not different for each customer. Everyone scans the same one and reaches the same page. The personal element is the link in the SMS, which concerns the particular appointment.

And it does not open the business's board. That has its own, signed link.
