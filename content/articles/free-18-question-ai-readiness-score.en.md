**Short answer:** Yes. NexAIpla's [AI Readiness Score](https://www.nexaipla.com/ai-score/) is free and asks for no signup. You answer 18 multiple-choice questions, divided among six dimensions, and see at once a score from 0 to 100 with a colour, the score for each dimension, and the likely inefficiencies worth fixing first. The scoring is done in your browser. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

The owner of a small business faces two bad choices. To buy something because everybody is talking about it and discover afterwards that it does not fit. Or to do nothing because they do not know where to begin. A short self-assessment test is the cheapest way out of that dilemma.

## What you get without paying

A number from 0 to 100, which sums up where you stand.

A colour, red, amber, or green, which tells you at a glance which band you belong to.

Your score in each of the six dimensions, so that it shows where you are strong and where not.

And an indication of the likely inefficiencies worth fixing first.

All of it appears straight after the last answer.

## What it asks

It does not ask about technology in the way you fear. It will not require you to know what a language model is.

It asks about your business, in six areas with three questions each.

Whether you have in mind a particular job you want help with, or only a general curiosity.

Where your records are and in what state.

Whether there are people with the time and the will to learn a new tool.

Whether daily tasks follow steady steps.

Whether the programs you use communicate with each other.

And who takes the decisions when something changes.

They are questions anyone who knows how the shop runs can answer.

## How you answer

Each question has ready answers, and you choose the one that best describes the present state. Each answer corresponds to 0 to 3 points.

You write no text and upload no documents. You need prepare nothing.

Eighteen taps, and you are done. It takes a few minutes, on a phone, between two customers.

## Who should take it

The person who knows how the business really works. Usually the owner, but not always. In some businesses, the office manager knows better where the files are.

Better still: two people, separately. If the results differ a lot, the difference deserves a conversation.

## What "free" does not hide

No email is asked for in order for the result to appear.

You create no account.

There is no trial period that expires, nor a "full report" unlocked by payment.

Your answers are not transmitted. The only exception is if you yourself submit a contact message, in which case the message is prefilled with your score and your weakest areas. More in [where my answers go](/en/articles/readiness-score-data-stays-in-browser).

## Why it is offered free

Because it serves both sides. A business that knows where it falls short asks better questions, and does not ask for a tool it is not ready for.

We prefer someone to write "I scored 45, with low data; where do I start?" than "I want AI".

## What it is not

It is not a consulting report. Eighteen questions do not replace someone who will look at your business up close.

It is neither an audit nor a certification. The result has no official standing and is not meant for showing to third parties.

It does not tell you which product to buy. It tells you what is worth fixing first.

And it is not an objective measurement. It rests on what you yourself declare, and is worth as much as the honesty with which you answer.

## What you do with the result

It depends on the colour. In red, the work is on the foundations. In amber, a narrow first use and the closing of one gap. In green, choosing the task of greatest value. The bands are analysed in [what the three colours mean](/en/articles/red-amber-green-readiness-bands).

In every case, note the score and the date. The test's value multiplies when you repeat it after some months and see what moved.

## Before you start, one piece of advice

Answer quickly and without embellishing. The first answer that comes to you is usually the true one. If you pause to think "technically, we do have something like that", you probably do not.
