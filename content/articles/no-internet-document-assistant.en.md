> **Quick answer:** Yes. [DocsApp](https://www.nexaipla.com/docsapp/) is installed once with a connection and after that works with no internet at all. The AI models are files on your laptop's disk and run through Ollama, the document index is local as well, and the chat opens in the browser without going out to the network. On a building site, on a ship, or in a basement, it answers the same way. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://nexaipla.com) (nexaipla.com).

All the well-known AI assistants share a weakness that the advertisements do not mention: without a connection they are an empty screen. The model sits in a data centre thousands of kilometres away, and every question is a trip there and back. When the trip cannot be made, there is no answer.

## Where there is no internet

In more places than anyone remembers from an office in town.

On the building site, especially in basements and behind reinforced concrete.

On the ship, where the satellite link is expensive, slow, and capped.

On the plane and on the train between two cities.

In rural areas and mountain villages, where the line drops with the wind.

On clients' premises where you are not given access to their network.

And in places that are deliberately cut off for security reasons, where no computer is allowed to see outwards.

## Why this application needs no connection

Because nothing it needs is anywhere else.

The language model is a large file on your disk. Ollama loads it into the computer's memory and runs it on the processor.

The document index is on the disk too. It was built when you dropped the files into the intake folder.

The interface opens in the browser, at 127.0.0.1:8080. That address means "this computer here". The browser needs no network to find it.

There is no licence check that "phones home" and no account that must be verified. The application starts and answers.

## What needs a connection, and when

Installation. The programs and the models are downloaded once. The models are several gigabytes, so do it at the office, on a good line, and not the night before from a hotel.

From then on, nothing. If you want a newer version at some point, you will fetch it the same way.

## Preparation before you leave

Whatever you do not take with you, you will not have. Five things are worth checking.

The documents. Drop them into the intake folder and wait for indexing to finish before closing the laptop. Large files and scans take their time.

A test with the Wi-Fi off. Ask two questions before setting out. If it works at the office with no network, it will work on the mountain.

Character recognition, if you have scans. Tesseract must be installed beforehand, with Greek and English.

The charger. A model running on the processor works it at full load. The battery drains much faster than when you are typing text.

The originals. The answer cites the document. You will want to be able to open it.

## What you can do in the field

Whatever you do at the office.

Question the technical specifications: "What insulation thickness does the study provide for the roof?"

Question the contract: "Who is responsible for removing the rubble?"

Get a summary of a maintenance manual.

Ask an Excel file for the total quantities per phase, or for a chart.

Every answer shows its source. And when the information is not in the documents, the application says so, which matters even more when you cannot phone anybody to confirm.

## New documents on the spot

Paper turns up in the field: a delivery note, a protocol, a note from the supervisor. You photograph or scan it, pass the file to the laptop by cable, and drop it into the intake folder. With character recognition it becomes text and enters the index, with no network needed at any step.

## What to expect of speed

Your laptop is not a data centre. With no graphics card, an answer takes from a few seconds to noticeably longer, depending on the machine and the question. In the field, where the alternative is leafing through a binder on a car bonnet, the comparison is favourable.

## The second gain

Whoever is looking for a solution for places with no signal also gets something they perhaps did not ask for: the documents go nowhere. The client's studies, the contracts, the drawings stay on the laptop. On premises where that is a requirement, the lack of a connection is the point and not the obstacle.
