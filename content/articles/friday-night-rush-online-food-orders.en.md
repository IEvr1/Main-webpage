Friday night is the most profitable and the most chaotic two hours of the week. Everybody decides not to cook at exactly the same moment. The shop that did twenty orders on Tuesday is asked to do seventy, with the same kitchen and the same phone.

## The bottleneck is not the grill

Most owners believe Friday's limit is the kitchen. Often it is not. The grill has room for ten more portions. What has no more room is the way orders get in.

One phone takes one call at a time. At three minutes an order, that is twenty orders an hour at best, if the person does nothing else. The twenty-first customer hears the engaged tone. They try once more. Then they open an app with other shops.

That lost order is written down nowhere. At the end of the night you see how many you did, not how many you did not pick up.

## What changes when the way in has no queue

The ordering page is never engaged. Ten customers can be building their carts at the same moment. Each takes as long as they like to choose, without keeping anyone else waiting.

And the person who held the handset goes back to production. On Friday that is a pair of hands on wrapping or packing, exactly at the hour they are missing.

## One queue that everybody sees

With seventy orders, the slips on the spike become a pile. One stays at the bottom.

On the dashboard the orders are cards in one live queue. Everybody sees the same sequence. When a customer cancels from their SMS, the card goes and no food is cooked for nobody. When they add something, the card changes before the bag is closed.

## The rush that is known at six

Scheduled orders are the most underrated tool for Friday. The customer who knows at midday that they want pizzas at nine can say so at midday.

For the kitchen that is a forecast. At six in the afternoon the shift leader sees that for eight to nine there are already fifteen orders. They know how much dough to take out and how much meat to skewer, and whether to call in the second driver.

Tell your regulars: "On Friday, order in the afternoon for the time you want." Those who do leave the tightest point of the evening.

## Drivers in the rush

On Friday the pass fills with bags and drivers come and go. Their own board shows which orders are ready, without their asking the kitchen.

When they declare that they have set off, the customer gets an SMS with an estimated arrival time. The evening on which deliveries run latest is also the evening on which that message saves the most calls.

If the trips are dragging, consider a smaller delivery radius for peak evenings. Fewer distant deliveries mean more nearby ones in the same hour.

## When the shop reaches its limit

There are evenings when the kitchen cannot keep up any more. The honest thing is to stop accepting, not to promise forty minutes and deliver in ninety.

When you declare a closure from the dashboard, the page stops taking new orders, and you finish those you have. And for the truly bad evening, such as a power cut or a broken oven, bulk cancel closes the open orders together, instead of fifteen phone calls of apology.

## What it does not solve

The system does not cook faster and does not add an oven. If your real limit is the burners, more orders will mean a longer wait, and you need to know that before opening the tap.

So look at the figures after the first two Fridays. If orders rose and the times held, the bottleneck was the phone. If the times got worse, it is time to look at preparation, staffing, or how many orders you can bear.

## A Friday in numbers

1. 18:00: the dashboard shows twelve scheduled for 20:00–21:00. The kitchen prepares ingredients before the evening warms up.
2. 20:30: nine orders arrive within ten minutes. The phone would have fitted three.
3. 21:45: the kitchen has twenty open cards. The owner declares a closure and finishes what is there, with no new promises.

## What the shop gains

- No order is lost because the line was engaged
- One more pair of hands in production at the peak hour
- Part of the demand is known from the afternoon
- There is a clean way to stop when you reach the limit

## How you prepare next Friday

1. Count how many orders you did last Friday and in which half hour they peaked.
2. From Wednesday, tell customers that they can schedule.
3. Look at the scheduled orders at 18:00 and prepare accordingly.
4. Decide beforehand at how many open cards you declare a closure.
5. On Saturday morning, compare the figures with the previous week.
