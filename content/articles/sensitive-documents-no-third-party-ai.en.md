**Short answer:** By running the model on your own computer. [DocsApp](https://www.nexaipla.com/docsapp/) uses local models through Ollama, so the documents and the questions never reach an outside AI provider. There is no third party processing the data, and no transfer outside your office. You remain responsible for the security of the computer and for who has access. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

The question "are we allowed to use AI?" has already been answered in most offices, only not by management. It was answered by the employee who had a forty-page report to summarise at five in the afternoon. They pasted it into a chatbot. It worked. They did it again.

## What happens when you paste

The text leaves your computer and reaches a company's servers. From that moment, that company is processing data on your behalf.

If the text contains personal data, that is, names, salaries, diagnoses, or identity numbers, you have just disclosed personal data to a third party. The data protection regulation asks, for such a thing, for a processing agreement, knowledge of where the servers are, and a lawful basis. The five-o'clock employee checked none of those.

If the text is confidential to a client, you have just shown it to someone the client does not know about.

## Which documents are "sensitive"

More than people think.

Staff documents: employment contracts, payrolls, appraisals, sick leave.

Medical ones: opinions, histories, test results.

Clients' financial ones: returns, bank transactions, balance sheets.

Legal ones: case files, contracts with a confidentiality clause, correspondence with opposing parties.

Trade secrets: suppliers' price lists, quotations, customer lists.

If you would not leave a document on a café table, it belongs here.

## A ban is not enough

The first reaction is an instruction: "Using AI on client documents is forbidden." It is right and it will not be kept.

The reason is simple. The tool saves real time, and the employee is under real pressure. A ban with no alternative drives the use onto the personal phone, where you do not see it at all.

What works is giving a permitted way that does the same job.

## What changes when the model is yours

The model runs on the office computer. The document is read there, and the question is answered there.

There is no AI provider in the middle. That simplifies a great deal. No processing agreement with an AI company is needed, because no AI company processes anything. No question of transfer to another country arises, because nothing is transferred. You do not need to read terms of use to see whether data is used for training, because nobody else has it.

NexAIpla does not receive your documents or your questions. There is no account and no sync.

## What remains your responsibility

Honesty is needed here. That the third party has left the picture does not mean obligations are over. It means only yours are left.

Security of the computer. Password, screen lock, disk encryption, updates. An unlocked computer with staff files is a problem with or without AI.

Access. Whoever sits at the machine sees whatever has been loaded. If not everyone should see the payroll, the application with the payroll does not go on the shared computer.

Retention. The application cleans up data automatically after six months, which helps with the principle of "no longer than necessary". You manage the originals under your own rules.

Purpose. That you can question a file does not mean you have a reason to. The rules on who sees what apply as before.

This text is not legal advice. If you have a data protection officer or a legal adviser, show them how the tool works and ask their opinion.

## How your technician verifies it

The claim "nothing is sent" can be tested. Disconnect the network and ask a question: if it is answered, the processing was local. Your technician can also watch the computer's connections while the question is being asked.

That test is worth recording. It is the answer you will give if a client or an auditor asks how you know.

## The office policy in three lines

Documents with personal or confidential data are not pasted into online AI services.

For those documents the local tool is used, on the designated computer.

Every answer is checked against its citation before it is used.

Three lines that are kept are worth more than ten pages nobody reads.

## Three cases

1. A medical practice: the secretary asks the histories for a patient's vaccination dates, on the practice's computer. No history leaves the building.
2. A payroll office: the manager asks the contracts about overtime terms, instead of pasting excerpts into a chatbot.
3. An accounting office: the technician records the test with the network disconnected, and the office shows it to a client who asked.

## What you gain

- No AI provider receives the documents or the questions
- No processing agreement with an AI company is needed
- Staff have a permitted way to do the same job
- The claim is verified with the network disconnected

## How you apply it in the office

1. Ask staff, without reprimand, which AI tools they use today.
2. Define which categories of document count as sensitive.
3. Install the local tool on one protected computer.
4. Run the test without a network and record it.
5. Write the policy in three lines and explain it to everyone.
