In Cyprus and in Greece a large share of a restaurant's customers do not read Greek: tourists, students, workers from other countries, permanent residents who have lived here for years. At the table the problem is solved with a smile and a finger on the menu. On the phone it is not solved.

## Why the phone shuts foreign customers out

A phone order in a foreign language is hard for both sides. The customer does not know how "sheftalia" is pronounced. The employee is not sure whether they heard "fifteen" or "fifty". The address is dictated letter by letter.

Most do not even try. They look for something they can read and tap.

## What a bilingual menu means

Every dish has a name and a description in Greek and in English. The customer switches language and sees the same list, with the same categories and the same prices.

It is not two menus. It is one menu with two faces. When you change a price, it changes in both. When a dish runs out, it is missing from both. There is no danger of the English leaflet that still has last year's prices.

The rest of the route is bilingual too: the cart, the choice of pickup or delivery, the map, sending.

## What reaches the kitchen

The cook does not need to know English. The "Pork souvlaki pitta" the customer tapped is the same item on the list as «πίτα σουβλάκι χοιρινό». The kitchen sees dishes and quantities, not a translation to be deciphered.

The only thing that arrives in the customer's language is their notes, because that is their own free text. "No onion" is easy. For rarer requests, it helps to have the usual options on the menu as separate dishes or descriptions, so that the customer does not need to write much.

## How a good English menu is written

The name of the dish stays. "Sheftalia" does not become "Cypriot sausage" in the title. The visitor came to eat sheftalia, and will want to find it again by name.

The description explains. Under the name, one line saying what it is: minced pork with onion and parsley, wrapped and grilled over charcoal. That is what makes someone order a thing they have never seen.

The ingredients that matter are stated outright. Pork, nuts, dairy, gluten. A customer who does not eat pork for religious reasons, or who has an allergy, must see it in their language, not guess.

Machine translation always needs checking by a person. Lamb chops that came out as "little children" are funny in a photo on the internet and not funny at all on your own menu.

## The visitor's phone

The tourist has a foreign number. The system accepts European mobile numbers, so they receive the confirmation SMS and the manage link just as a local does. They do not need to make an account for an order they will place twice during their holiday.

## Where the visitor finds the link

Where they are: at the hotel, in the rented flat, on the beach. A QR code at the reception of a nearby guesthouse, on the leaflet you leave in flats, on the shop door. And on your map profile, where anyone who does not know the area looks first.

Write two words in English beside the code: "Order online". Without them, the visitor does not know what they will find on scanning.

## Who benefits besides tourists

Permanent residents from other countries, who are customers all year and not only in summer. Foreign students near universities. Companies with international staff ordering lunch for ten.

And Greek-speaking customers lose nothing. The menu opens in Greek, as always.

## Three orders

1. A couple from Germany scans the QR code in the flat, reads what kleftiko is, and orders delivery with a German mobile.
2. An office with an international team sends lunch for eight, and each person read the menu in their own language.
3. The kitchen sees "2 × sheftalia, 1 × village salad, no onion" and does not pause for a second.

## What the shop gains

- Customers who would never phone order by themselves
- One menu to maintain, with prices that agree in both languages
- The kitchen works with the dishes on the list, with no translation
- Allergens and ingredients are visible in the customer's language

## How you prepare the bilingual menu

1. For each dish, write a name and one line of description in Greek.
2. Translate the descriptions into English and keep the local names.
3. Give the English text to someone who speaks the language well, for checking.
4. Place a test order from the English face and see how it showed in the kitchen.
5. Put QR codes with an English label where visitors are.
