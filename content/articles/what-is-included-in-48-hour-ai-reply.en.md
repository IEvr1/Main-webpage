> **Quick answer:** Four things. When you describe a need to [Custom AI Tools](https://www.nexaipla.com/ai/), within 48 hours you receive: a feasibility percentage, an indicative cost range, an estimate of the time to build, and recommended next steps. The reply commits you to nothing. It is a reasoned first opinion, written to be read by an owner and not an engineer. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://nexaipla.com) (nexaipla.com).

The owner of a small business does not need a lecture on artificial intelligence. They need three answers before spending an hour or a euro: can it be done? roughly what does it cost? when shall I have it? The 48-hour reply exists to give them, along with a fourth that nobody usually asks for but is the most useful.

## The feasibility percentage

A number, on a scale of a hundred. It expresses how confident we are that what you described can be done and work reliably, with today's technology and with the facts of your own business.

A high percentage means the task is one of those that automate well: repetitive, with clear rules, with information that already exists in digital form.

A middling percentage usually comes with a condition. "It can be done, provided orders arrive in writing" or "it can be done for eight cases in ten, and the rest need a person".

A low percentage is an answer too, and saves you money. Some jobs rest on judgement, on relationships, or on information that is not written down anywhere.

The number always comes with its reasoning. A percentage with no explanation would not help you decide.

## The indicative cost range

A range, not a price. From so much to so much.

There is a reason for the spread. A description of a few paragraphs does not show everything. How clean your records are, how many exceptions the process has, which programs the tool must connect to. The lower end of the range corresponds to the simple scenario and the upper to the complicated one.

The range is enough for the first decision: is this something my business can consider, or not? If the lower end is already above what the problem is worth to you, you know in two days and not after three meetings.

To weigh cost against benefit, the method in [how the return on an automation is calculated](/en/articles/calculate-automation-roi) helps.

## The time estimate

How many weeks from "we start" to you using it.

It is an estimate, and it depends on you as well. A tool that needs examples from your own documents moves as fast as you supply them. In small businesses, the owner's time is usually the bottleneck, not the programming.

## The recommended next steps

This is the fourth part, and often the most valuable. It does not only say "it can be done". It says what we would do in your place.

It may be "start with a small piece of the job and see whether it serves you". It may be "first gather the records in one place". It may be "a ready-made application already covers this; you need nothing special".

It may also be "do not do it now". If the task is done five times a month, automating it rarely pays, and it is better to hear that from us.

## What the reply is not

It is not a contract. It is not a binding offer. It is not a detailed specification, nor a demonstration.

It does not oblige you to a next step. You can read it, discuss it with your partner, and never get in touch again.

## Why 48 hours

Fast enough that you still remember why you asked. Slow enough that somebody has thought about your case and not a general one.

An owner who waits three weeks for a first opinion has already gone back to the daily round, and the idea has been forgotten.

## What makes the reply better

The quality of the reply follows the quality of the description. Four things help more than any others.

The type and size of the business. A tutoring centre with three teachers is not an accounting office with twenty staff.

The problem, with numbers if you have them. "We enter about sixty invoices a week by hand and it takes four hours."

What success looks like. "It does it by itself and I just check."

The tools you already use. Even if they are a spreadsheet and a messaging app.

You can write these in the form or say them in a voice message.

## After the reply

The decision is yours. If you want to go ahead, the reply becomes the basis of the conversation. If you have questions about the percentage or the range, you ask. If you decide it is not the time, you at least have a picture you did not have before.

## Three replies, three directions

1. An accounting office asks about automatic sorting of vouchers. High feasibility, a cost range, a few weeks, and a suggestion to start with a single document type.
2. A shop asks about automatic answers to customer questions. Middling feasibility, on condition that prices and terms are first written down in one place.
3. A craftsman asks about automatic costing of bespoke constructions. Low feasibility, and a suggestion not to do it, since every job is different.

## What the reply gives you

- A number for whether it can be done, with its reasoning
- An order of magnitude for cost before any meeting
- An estimate of when you will be using it
- An honest recommendation, even when it is "not now"

## How you get a useful reply

1. Choose one task, not five.
2. State the type and size of your business.
3. Describe the problem with frequency and time.
4. Say what success will look like and which tools you already have.
5. Send it from the form or by voice message and wait 48 hours.
