**Short answer:** A pharmacy shows customers how busy it is with [Live Business Traffic](https://www.nexaipla.com/shoptraffic/). The pharmacist or an assistant taps one of four colour-coded levels on a private screen, and the public page shows it together with the opening hours, duty days, and announcements. Customers need no account, and the page holds no health information of any kind. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

A pharmacy queue is not like a café queue. The people standing in it are often unwell, tired, or holding a child with a fever. Many could come an hour later with no harm at all, if they knew that in an hour the counter would be quiet. Nobody tells them, so they come when everyone else comes.

## When a pharmacy fills up

The peaks are predictable to the pharmacist and invisible to everyone else. Monday morning. The half hour after the nearby doctors finish their surgeries. The first days of the month, when repeat prescriptions are renewed. The evening when you are the pharmacy on duty and the whole district arrives at one door.

Between those peaks there are long quiet stretches. A customer who wants sunscreen, vitamins, or something they collect every month has no reason to stand behind six urgent prescriptions. The live level lets that customer choose the quiet stretch without asking.

Then there is the closed afternoon of the week. When the pharmacies around you are shut and you are open, or the other way round, the page shows it plainly and nobody has to stand in front of lowered shutters to find out.

## What goes on the page, and what never does

The page shows four things: the busy level in colour, whether the pharmacy is open now, the weekly hours with special closed days, and an announcement. In a pharmacy the announcement line works every day: "On duty tonight until 23:00", "Closed on Wednesday afternoon", "Flu vaccines available, ask at the counter".

What it never shows is anything about a person. There are no names, no prescriptions, no medicines tied to anyone, and customers do not log in anywhere. The level is a statement about the room, like a sign in the window that can be read from home. That keeps the page a long way from health data.

## Who taps, and when

A pharmacist checking a dosage should not be thinking about a phone. Give the tap to the assistant at the till, or tie it to a fixed moment: every time the queue changes shape, and always at the start of each known peak. The owner link opens with no shared password and can stay on the home screen of the shop tablet.

Set the levels by how many people are waiting, not by how tired the team feels. LOW: nobody waiting. MEDIUM: up to three. BUSY: a line to the door. VERY BUSY: a line outside as well, as on a duty night.

## Three everyday scenes

1. A daughter collecting her mother's monthly medicines checks the page at 09:15, sees BUSY, and comes at 11:00 on LOW. Her mother waits in the car for three minutes instead of twenty.
2. On a duty Sunday the pharmacy writes "On duty today 08:00–23:00" in the announcement. People who would have phoned to confirm find the answer on the link from Google.
3. The pharmacist closes for fifteen minutes to receive a special delivery, taps the emergency close, and reopens with one more tap.

## What the page is not for

It does not take orders for medicines, it does not put a product aside, and it does not say whether something is on the shelf. It does not book a time for a blood-pressure check or a vaccination either. If the pharmacy offers services by appointment, those belong on [Online Receptionist 24/7](https://www.nexaipla.com/onlinebooking/), where the customer picks a time and receives an SMS. The busy page is for everyone who simply walks in.

## What the pharmacy gains

- Customers who are not in a hurry choose the quiet hours themselves
- Fewer calls while prescriptions are being checked
- Duty days and special hours published where people look first
- No personal or medical data on the page

## How a pharmacy sets it up

1. List your regular peaks: days, hours, duty shifts.
2. Have the page set up with the weekly hours and the closed afternoons of your area.
3. Put the owner link on the till tablet and give the tap to the assistant.
4. Add the public link to the Google profile and to the door sign, next to the duty rota.
5. Update the announcement on every duty day and remove it the next morning.
