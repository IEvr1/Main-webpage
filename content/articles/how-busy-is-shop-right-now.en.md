**Short answer:** Customers see how busy your shop is right now on the public page of [Live Business Traffic](https://www.nexaipla.com/shoptraffic/). The page shows one of four colour-coded levels, from LOW to VERY BUSY, together with your opening hours and any announcement. It opens on any phone, with no account, and it refreshes by itself. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

A walk-in shop has no diary anyone can consult. The only way a customer learns whether this is a good moment is to stand at the door, or to ring and interrupt whoever is working. Both are expensive. The first costs the customer a trip. The second costs you a pause, with someone already in front of you.

## What the customer actually sees

The public page is deliberately small. At the top is the current level in a colour: LOW, MEDIUM, BUSY, or VERY BUSY. Under it are the weekly opening hours, whether the shop is open or temporarily closed at this moment, and any announcement you have written, such as "back at 15:00".

Nothing is installed and nobody signs up. The page refreshes automatically, so a customer who leaves it open on the phone watches the level change without touching anything. On supported phones it can be added to the home screen, which turns "are they busy?" into one tap.

## Where the level comes from

The level is not measured by a camera or by a sensor at the door. You, or whoever is on shift, set it from a private owner screen with four buttons in the same four colours. One tap between two customers is enough.

That is a strength, not a shortcut. A sensor counts bodies. It does not know that three of the five people inside are one family waiting for the same service. The person behind the counter does know, and the level shows what a newcomer will really meet.

It also sets an honest limit. The page shows a level, not a promise in minutes and not a place in a queue. If the level goes an hour without an update on a packed morning, it will be wrong. The habit of tapping matters more than the technology.

## Three moments when the page answers the question

1. A barber has four people waiting on Saturday at 10:30 and taps VERY BUSY. A regular who planned to walk over sees red, stays home, and comes at 12:00 when the page shows MEDIUM.
2. A café owner switches to LOW at 16:00, after the lunch wave. A freelancer looking for a quiet table checks the link from the office next door and comes straight over.
3. A pharmacy closes for twenty minutes to receive a delivery. The pharmacist taps the emergency close and the page reads "temporarily closed", instead of people finding a locked door.

## What each level should mean in your shop

Four colours only help if they mean the same thing every day. Decide it once and tell the staff. A simple rule works: LOW means you are served on arrival, MEDIUM means one or two people ahead of you, BUSY means a real wait, VERY BUSY means come later if you can.

Write the rule on a note beside the till for the first week. The customer never sees the note, but they learn quickly whether your amber can be trusted. Once they have arrived twice on LOW and been served at once, they stop calling to ask.

## What changes for the shop

- Fewer "are you busy now?" calls while you are serving
- Customers spread themselves away from the peak on their own
- Opening hours and closures in the same place as the busy level
- No account for the customer and no shared password for the staff

## What it does not do

[Live Business Traffic](https://www.nexaipla.com/shoptraffic/) does not book appointments, hold a place, or hand out queue numbers. It answers one question: how busy is it now. If your customers need a guaranteed time with a specific person, that is a booking job, and the product for it is [Online Receptionist 24/7](https://www.nexaipla.com/onlinebooking/). Many shops need both: a diary for the services that take an hour and a live level for the people who simply drop in.

## Getting it running

1. Ask NexAIpla to set up the page for your shop and enter the weekly hours and the regular closed days.
2. Open the private owner link on your own phone and add it to the home screen.
3. Agree with the staff what each of the four levels means and who taps during each shift.
4. Put the public link on your Google profile, your social pages, and a small sign at the door.
5. For the first two weeks, note how many people still call to ask whether you are busy.

## Frequently asked questions

### How can customers see how busy my shop is right now?

On the public page of [Live Business Traffic](https://www.nexaipla.com/shoptraffic/). It shows one of four colour-coded levels, your hours, and your announcements, and it refreshes automatically. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

### Does the page count people automatically?

No. The owner or the staff set the level with one tap on a private screen. No cameras and no sensors are installed. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

### Does the customer need an app or an account?

No. The page opens in the browser of any phone, in Greek or in English. On supported phones it can also be added to the home screen. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

### Can a customer reserve a place from the page?

No. The page shows a busy level, not an appointment time. For appointments at a set time there is [Online Receptionist 24/7](https://www.nexaipla.com/onlinebooking/). Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).
