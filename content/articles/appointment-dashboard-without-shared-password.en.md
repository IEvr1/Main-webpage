> **Quick answer:** With a signed link. In [Online Receptionist 24/7](https://www.nexaipla.com/onlinebooking/) the appointment board has no login screen with a username and password. It opens from an address that contains a digital signature, which the system checks. Whoever has the link sees appointments, figures, and closures. Whoever does not have it cannot guess it. There is no password to be forgotten or passed around. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://nexaipla.com) (nexaipla.com).

In a small business the shared password is not negligence. It is the only practical solution when five people use one computer and nobody has time for personal accounts. The problem is what follows.

## What goes wrong with the shared password

It is easy, because everybody has to remember it. The shop's name and a year.

It is written down somewhere, because someone always forgets it.

It never changes, because changing it means telling everybody, and someone will be locked out on Saturday morning.

People who no longer work there know it.

And often it is the same password used elsewhere, on the shop's email or with a supplier.

Inside the appointment program are customers' names and phone numbers. That is not something you want behind "salon2019".

## What the signed link is

An address, long and meaningless to the eye. Part of it is a signature produced by the system. When you open the link, the system checks that the signature is genuine and shows you the board.

You type nothing. There is no "I forgot my password".

The signature is not guessed the way a password is guessed. Nobody will find it by trying the shop's name.

## What it opens

The business's board: appointments by day and person, the figures, the closures, and the emergency reschedule for when somebody is unexpectedly absent.

It is the shop's side. Customers never see it.

## Three links, three jobs

It is worth not confusing them.

The booking page is public. You share it everywhere and put it in a QR code in the window. It shows free times and nothing else.

The link in the customer's SMS concerns one appointment. With it the customer cancels or changes their own, and only that.

The board link belongs to the business. It is not shared publicly.

## Who needs the board link

Fewer people than you think.

The owner, and whoever does reception. They need the overall picture and the tools.

The other team members usually need only their own schedule. They see that in their Google Calendar, where their appointments are synced. They need not have the board link at all.

That separation makes the biggest difference. With the shared password, everyone saw everything because there was no other way to see their day.

## Treat it like a key

The link opens the board for whoever has it. So it matters where it is.

Save it as a bookmark on the reception computer or tablet, and as a shortcut on your phone's home screen.

Do not send it to the staff group chat. Those chats have members who left two years ago.

Do not forward it by email to an accountant, a supplier, or a friend "to see what it looks like".

And lock the screen of the devices where it is saved. An unlocked tablet on the counter is an open door.

## When someone leaves

With the password, the right move was to change it, and it almost never happened.

With the link, if the person who left had it, you ask us for a new one. The old one stops working, and you give the new one to the two or three who need it. If they never had it, because they only looked at their calendar, you need do nothing beyond removing them from the team.

The same applies if a phone or tablet is lost.

## From which devices

From any that has a browser. The reception computer, a tablet, your phone at home in the evening.

You install no program. That also means there is nothing to update or set up again when you change device. You open the link on the new one.

## What to expect and what not

The link solves the problem of the password that gets around. It does not replace your judgement about who should have access.

Whoever holds the link sees what the board shows. So the rule is simple: few people, known devices.

If your business needs something more elaborate, such as a different view for each branch, tell us at setup.

## Three situations

1. A salon of five: the link is on the reception tablet and on the owner's phone. The others see their day in their calendar.
2. A receptionist leaves. The owner asks for a new link and saves it again on the two devices.
3. A garage owner opens the board from his phone on Sunday to declare that Monday will be closed.

## What you gain

- No password on a note, no password to forget
- Board access only for those who need it
- Staff see their schedule without entering the board
- One person's departure leaves no access open

## How you organise access

1. Decide which two or three people need the board.
2. Save the link as a bookmark on their devices.
3. Connect the others' Google Calendar for their personal schedule.
4. Put a screen lock on every device that holds the link.
5. Request a replacement link whenever a receptionist leaves or a tablet goes missing.
