> **Quick answer:** With the owner dashboard of [Online Food Orders](https://www.nexaipla.com/foodorder/). Every order a customer sends appears in a live queue with dishes, quantities, notes, pickup or delivery, and the time. The same screen has the day's key figures, the shop closures, the bulk cancel for emergencies, and the settings. It opens through a secure link, without a password. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://nexaipla.com) (nexaipla.com).

In a small kitchen the "order" of the orders lives in three places: on a spike with slips of paper, in the grill cook's head, and on the owner's phone. While orders are few, the three agree. When they become many, a slip falls behind the counter and the customer phones forty minutes later.

## One queue instead of three

The dashboard has one list, and everyone looks at the same one. Each order is a card with what the kitchen needs: which dishes, how many, what the customer asked for in the notes, whether it is for pickup or delivery, and for what time.

The list is live. When a customer sends an order, it appears without anyone refreshing the page. When the customer changes or cancels it from their SMS, the card changes or goes. There is no chance of cooking something that was cancelled ten minutes ago because the message went to another phone.

## What the card says and why it matters

Dishes are written with the names on your menu, not the way the customer said them. "A mixed grill, the big one, you know" becomes the exact dish on the list.

The notes are the customer's own words. "No tzatziki, allergy" did not pass from ear to hand to paper. It was written once, by the person who means it.

The time separates orders for now from scheduled ones. An order for nine in the evening that was sent at six does not need to go on the heat at five past six.

## The day's figures

Above the queue are the shop's key figures. They are not a report for an accountant. They are the glance the owner takes at ten at night to understand how the shift went, without counting slips.

Their value shows after a few weeks. Which evenings are really busy? Is the second driver worth it on a Tuesday? With slips, those questions are answered by feeling. With figures, they are answered by numbers.

## Closures and emergencies

The screen does not only show. It also controls when you accept orders.

Closures state when the shop is not working: a public holiday, a private event, two weeks' leave in August. The customer sees it on the page and does not send an order to a closed kitchen.

Bulk cancel is for the evening when something breaks: the power went, the oven failed, the driver is stuck on the road. Instead of phoning twelve customers one by one, you cancel the open orders together. It is a tool you hope not to need, but the one time it is needed it saves an hour of phone calls and many angry customers.

## Access without a password

The dashboard opens through a secure, signed link. There is no username and password written on paper next to the tablet. Whoever has the link gets in, and whoever does not have it does not.

Drivers do not use this link. They have their own board with its own link, showing only the deliveries. So the driver does not see settings and figures, and the owner's link is not circulating on five phones.

## Where the screen goes

A tablet on a stand, at eye level, where the spike with the slips sits today. Away from the fryer, near a socket. In shops with two stations, the same queue can be open both at the pass and at the till.

Those who still want paper on the plate can go on writing a mark on the packaging. The difference is that the source of truth is now the screen, and the paper is merely a label.

## Three moments

1. 20:15: seven open cards. The grill cook sees that three have gyros and cooks them together instead of three times.
2. 20:40: a customer cancels from the SMS. The card disappears before the dough is opened.
3. 21:30: the oven fails. The owner bulk-cancels the open orders and declares a closure for the rest of the evening.

## What the kitchen gains

- One list that everybody sees, instead of slips and memory
- Changes and cancellations show the moment they are made
- The owner sees the shift in numbers
- In an emergency, customers are notified together and not one by one

## How you put it to work

1. Decide who on the shift is responsible for the screen.
2. Set the tablet in a fixed place and open the secure link.
3. Place five test orders, with a change and a cancellation, before opening to the public.
4. Enter the month's closures now.
5. Look at the figures every Monday and note what you change.
