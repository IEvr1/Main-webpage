The client books a service and a stylist in chat or by QR, with no account, and receives an SMS reminder with a link to cancel or move. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

Salon owners ask this while standing at a basin. The book is at the desk. The person who understands it has foils in their hands. Instagram already has the client. The missing piece is a yes or a no on a real slot, not another “I’ll check and message you”.

## Why salon diaries break on Saturdays

A haircut and a colour are not the same square on a grid. If every booking is “one hour”, a balayage overruns the next three people. If two staff share one notebook, the nail technician is promised twice because the hair team did not see that page.

No-shows hurt more on Saturday than on Tuesday. The chair rate is higher and the waiting list was real at 10:00. A reminder that only says “see you tomorrow” does not give the client a button to free the chair. The SMS link does: confirm, cancel, or move, without a phone call while you are mid-service.

Clients already decide on Google and Instagram. A booking tool that lives only inside software your client must download will not be used at 22:00. The page has to open in the browser they already have.

## What to switch on, and what to ignore

Use [Online Receptionist 24/7](https://www.nexaipla.com/onlinebooking/).

Switch on:

- Services with true durations.
- A column per stylist.
- Chat and a QR at the desk.
- SMS confirmation and reminder.
- Google Calendar for each stylist who already lives in a personal calendar.

Ignore, for this question:

- A generic chatbot that chats about hair trends and never writes the diary.
- A marketplace that takes a commission and sends you people who are shopping ten salons. This is your page.
- [Live Business Traffic](https://www.nexaipla.com/shoptraffic/), unless you also take walk-ins and people ask “how long is the wait right now?”. That page does not book a colour for next Thursday.

## How a salon client should experience it

1. She taps the link in the Instagram bio.
2. She chooses “colour” and sees only stylists who do colour, with gaps long enough for the service.
3. She books without creating a password.
4. She gets an SMS. The night before, the same link is the reminder.
5. If her plans change, she moves the visit herself. You see the gap while it can still be filled.

Staff open the board with a signed link. They do not share one password on a sticky note. Closures — a sick stylist, a day off — come off the public page so nobody books a person who is not there.

## What a salon should prepare before the first booking

| Item | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Duration of each service | Stops a colour being squeezed into a cut slot |
| Which stylist does which service | Stops a junior appearing as free for a correction |
| Days you refuse late slots | A 20:30 gap you will resent should not be bookable |
| The one link you will publish | Instagram, Google, and the desk QR must match |

Run five test bookings: cut, colour, a cancel, a move, and a closure. If those five behave, clients can have the link.

## Menu mistakes that make the diary lie

A salon link fails when the menu lies. If “colour” is booked as 45 minutes, the afternoon collapses and the staff blame the software. Write the duration you actually need, including the time the client sits while colour processes. If a junior cannot do corrections, do not show that junior as free for corrections.

Tell clients the new rule once, in the same place as the link: the book is the link, not a DM that says “I think I’m free”. For the first two Saturdays, keep one person watching the board so a sick stylist is closed before anyone books them. After those two Saturdays, the SMS cancel link should be doing the no-show work that used to be a phone call at the door.
