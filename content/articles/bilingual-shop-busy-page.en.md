**Short answer:** Yes. The public page of [Live Business Traffic](https://www.nexaipla.com/shoptraffic/) is available in Greek and in English. Visitors, foreign residents, and locals read the same busy level, opening hours, and "open" or "closed" status in the language they choose, with no account. The four levels are colour-coded, so the main message is clear before a single word is read. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

In Limassol, Paphos, Larnaca, on the islands, in the centre of Athens, a shop's customers do not share one language. The pharmacy beside the marina, the barber near the university, the café in the old town serve people who read Greek and people who do not. The phone call from the second group is the hardest one in a rush, because it lasts longer and both sides are guessing.

## What is bilingual

The interface of the public page is bilingual: the labels of the level, the status that says whether the shop is open, the days of the week with their hours, the headings. The visitor switches language on the page, and the shop does not have to maintain two separate pages or two links.

The level itself is a colour first. Green and red need no translation. A visitor understands that it is a good or a bad moment before they have read LOW or VERY BUSY.

The language does not change the content. The local and the visitor see the same level and the same hours at the same moment. The English version cannot show yesterday's information, because it is the same page with different labels.

## What you must write in both languages yourself

The announcement is free text typed by the owner. Do not count on it translating itself. Write the short phrase in both languages on the same line: "Κλειστά 15 Αυγούστου / Closed 15 August".

Stay with dates and times, which travel easily from one language to another. "Ανοίγουμε 16:00 / Open 16:00" is understood by anyone who can read a clock. Avoid phrases such as "after lunch" or "around five", which neither translate cleanly nor say exactly when.

Keep a sheet beside the till with five ready wordings in the two languages: closed tomorrow, back in a few minutes, summer hours, open on the holiday, closing earlier today. Then nobody has to think in English in the middle of a rush.

## Colour as the third language

Many customers read neither Greek nor English comfortably. For them the colour and the figures do the work. A red panel and "09:00–13:00" are understood by everyone. That is why it pays to keep the level right and the announcement lean: the fewer the words, the more people understand it.

## Where it matters most

On the door. A visitor does not expect the midday break and stands puzzled in front of a closed shop at three. A QR with a caption in both languages tells them when you open.

In accommodation. Hotel receptions and holiday-rental hosts hear "is the pharmacy open?" all day. If they have your link, they answer with it.

On the Google profile. Visitors search in English and open whatever they find there.

And on the phone, when it rings all the same. Instead of explaining opening times in a language they are not comfortable in, the staff say four words everyone understands, "look at the page", and point to the QR or send the link by message.

## Set-up for a shop with foreign customers

1. Have the page set up and check both languages on your own phone.
2. Prepare standard announcement wordings in two languages and keep them beside the till.
3. Print a door QR with the caption "Πόσο κόσμο έχουμε τώρα / How busy we are now".
4. Send the link to nearby hotels or rental hosts if visitors are a large part of your trade.
5. Ask one English-speaking customer to read the page and tell you what was unclear.
