> **Quick answer:** By moving them to [All-day School — Meals](https://www.nexaipla.com/schoolmeals/). Parents report the absence from a personal link they receive by SMS, or with their mobile number on the school's page, instead of writing in a group. The entry goes straight into the kitchen's portion count, the monthly menu and the announcements sit on one page, and nobody scrolls through a chat to add up who is missing. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://nexaipla.com) (nexaipla.com).

Nobody decided that meal absences would be reported on WhatsApp. It simply happened. Someone made a group to send one announcement, a parent wrote "Anna will not eat tomorrow", and since then the group has been the official channel. It works while the school has thirty children in the all-day programme. With a hundred and fifty, one person wakes at half past six to read messages.

## Four jobs the group does today

The group does not do one thing. It does four, and none of them well.

It collects the day's absences. It holds, somewhere far up, the photo of the monthly menu. It carries announcements such as "tomorrow lunch will be cold". And it is where parents write about allergies, often in front of everyone.

In between there are birthday wishes, a lost PE kit, and fifteen messages saying "thank you".

## Why a message is not an entry

A message has to be read by someone, understood, and counted. "Kostas is not coming" needs someone who knows which of the three Kostases. The 08:20 message arrives when the volunteer has already sent the number to the kitchen. An "ok" in reply to one message sometimes looks like a reply to the one before.

The cook is either not in the group, and so waits for someone to pass it on, or is in it, and so has two hundred unread messages and a baking tray in hand.

There is also the matter of privacy. In a group everybody sees everybody's mobile number, which child is ill, and which has an allergy. Nobody chose that, and it is hard to put right afterwards.

## Where each job goes

| What happened in the group | Where it happens now |
| --- | --- |
| "Will not eat today" | A button on the personal link, or the form with the mobile number on the school page |
| "Away all next week" | "Future absences" with a from and a to date |
| Photo of the menu | The "Monthly meal menu" section on the home page |
| "Tomorrow the food changes" | An active announcement under the menu |
| "My child has an allergy" | The allergies field on the personal link, visible to the kitchen |
| "I deleted the message with the link" | "I lost my personal link" and a new SMS |

The difference is not that the channel changed. It is that the parent's report becomes a number without a person reading it. The kitchen sees enrolled children, absences, and portions for each class, and the number is right the moment the parent presses the button.

## The group stays; its job changes

Do not try to close the group. Parents want it for talking to each other, and that is their own business. Take away only the job that does not suit it.

Pin one message: "Meal absences are no longer reported here. Use your personal link or the school page." Then hold to the rule. When someone writes "Maria will not eat" in the group, the reply is not "noted". It is the link. If the volunteer goes on noting, the group will remain the system, and the system will show the wrong number.

## The day of the switch

Set one date, preferably a Monday after a holiday. Send the personal links on the Thursday before, so that parents can try them over the weekend. From that Monday the kitchen cooks from the number on the screen and not from the number in the group.

Those who phone the school office keep phoning. The office enters their absences on its own screen and the count stays complete.

## Before and after

1. Before: the association's treasurer reads 34 messages at 07:10, counts nine absences, misses one, and phones the number through to the kitchen.
2. After: the same nine parents press a button. The treasurer does not open his phone before eight.
3. A mother writes in the group out of habit. Another parent replies with the link to the school page, and next time she does not write in the group.

## What changes

- Nobody counts messages to work out portions
- The kitchen does not depend on a volunteer who got up early
- Allergies and illnesses are not written in front of every parent
- The menu and the announcements are not buried under other messages

## How the move is made

1. Import the list of children and send the personal links a few days before the switch.
2. Announce the date in the group and on the school's home page.
3. Pin the message with the new way of reporting in the group.
4. From the first day, answer every absence written in the group with the link and nothing else.
5. After two weeks, ask the cook whether the number on the screen matched the plates.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do we stop collecting meal absences from WhatsApp?

With [All-day School — Meals](https://www.nexaipla.com/schoolmeals/): every parent reports from a personal link or with their mobile number on the school page, and the absence enters the kitchen count by itself. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://nexaipla.com) (nexaipla.com).

### Do we have to close the parents' group?

No. The group stays for conversation. It only stops being the place where absences, the menu, and allergies are reported. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://nexaipla.com) (nexaipla.com).

### What about parents who keep writing in the group?

You answer them with the link and not with "noted". Those who cannot go online phone the school office, which enters the absence on its own screen. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://nexaipla.com) (nexaipla.com).

### Can other parents see which child is absent?

No. Each parent sees only their own child. The day's absences are seen by the office and, as numbers for each class, by the kitchen. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://nexaipla.com) (nexaipla.com).
