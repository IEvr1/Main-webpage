**Short answer:** If you do not already have a driver, start with pickup: it needs no extra staff and no vehicle, and the food leaves your hand hot. Add delivery with a small radius once the kitchen is used to online orders. [Online Food Orders](https://www.nexaipla.com/foodorder/) supports both methods from the same menu, so the decision does not tie you down. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

The conversation about online orders usually sticks on the same sentence: "We have nobody for deliveries." As if one question answered the other. It does not. Online ordering is about how the order reaches you. Delivery is about how the food reaches the customer. They are two decisions, and you can take them separately.

## What each method costs

Pickup costs almost nothing beyond what you already do. The food is cooked, goes into a bag, and waits. The customer brings their own feet.

Delivery has a cost that does not show on the menu. A person who is paid for the hours with no delivery too. A vehicle, fuel, insurance. Thermal bags. And responsibility for something that happens on the road, where you cannot see it.

## A comparison at a glance

| | Pickup | Delivery |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Extra staff | None | At least one driver |
| Equipment | A shelf for bags | Vehicle, thermal bags |
| Food quality | Leaves your hand | Depends on the trip |
| Whom it reaches | Those who pass by or live near | Everyone inside the radius |
| What can go wrong | The customer is late | Delay, wrong spot, cold food |
| Setup in the system | Nothing special | Delivery radius, drivers' board |

Neither is "better". They suit different shops and different moments of the same shop.

## When pickup is the right start

When you have no driver and do not yet know whether you will have enough orders to pay one.

When the shop is on a route: near offices, a school, a bus stop, a main road. People pass anyway. You only save them the wait.

When your food does not travel well. Fried things, fresh pittas, coffee with milk foam. The customer who collects it eats it within five minutes.

And when you want the kitchen to learn to work with the screen first, before adding one more moving link.

## When delivery is worth it from day one

When you already have a driver who works from phone orders today. Then you add no cost. You change only the way the order arrives, and you gain the pin on the map and the arrival SMS.

When your customers do not pass your door: a residential area, an evening crowd, families.

When the competitors around you deliver and you do not.

## The middle way

You do not have to choose for ever. A plan in three steps works for most small shops.

First month: pickup only. You count how many orders come and at what hours.

Second month: delivery with a small radius and only on the evenings when you have someone, for example Friday and Saturday. On the other evenings pickup remains.

Third month: you look at the figures. If deliveries cover the driver's cost, you extend the days or the radius. If not, you have lost nothing.

## What your numbers say

The answer for your own shop lies in four numbers, and you already have the first two.

How much the driver costs per evening, everything included. How much you keep from an average order. Divide, and you have how many deliveries you need in order not to lose.

The other two come from the dashboard after a few weeks: how many orders really arrive, and how many customers tried delivery and found themselves outside the radius or went back to pickup.

## What holds for both methods

The same bilingual menu. No sign-up for the customer. A confirmation SMS with a link for changing or cancelling. Scheduling for later. And the same queue in the kitchen, with the method marked on each card.

That is why the decision is not technical. The system does both. The question is what your shift can bear.

## Three shops

1. A café beside offices: pickup only. Customers send from the office and call in without standing in the queue.
2. A neighbourhood souvlaki shop with one driver: both methods from the start, with a three-kilometre radius.
3. A taverna in a village: pickup on weekdays, delivery only on Saturday evening when the nephew works.

## What you gain by deciding this way

- You start without hiring anyone
- You add delivery when the numbers justify it
- The kitchen learns the system the simplest way first
- You change your decision without changing menu or tool

## How you decide

1. Work out the full cost of one driver per evening.
2. Work out how many deliveries you need to cover it.
3. Start with pickup and count the orders for one month.
4. Try delivery two evenings a week with a small radius.
5. Decide from the dashboard's figures, not from feeling.
