**Short answer:** No. [Online Receptionist 24/7](https://www.nexaipla.com/onlinebooking/) is not a voice assistant and does not answer calls. Booking is done in writing: the customer opens the page from a link or a QR code, chooses service, person, and time through chat, and receives a confirmation and a reminder by SMS. Your phone goes on ringing for you, only less. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

The product's name creates a reasonable expectation. A receptionist, in most people's minds, is the one who lifts the handset. It is worth making clear from the start what it does and does not do, before you rest on it something it is not.

## What it does

It books appointments round the clock, in writing.

The customer reaches the booking page from a link or a QR code. Through a short chat they choose a service, a team member, and a time. Only times nobody else has taken are suggested.

With a mobile number they finish, and the confirmation comes by SMS. Before the appointment they get an automatic reminder. From the link in the messages they cancel or change the time.

You see everything on a board, and each team member in their Google Calendar.

## What it does not do

It does not pick up the phone.

It does not speak in a synthetic voice.

It does not call customers to remind them of the appointment.

It does not transcribe voicemail.

If someone calls the shop's number, the phone rings as always. You answer it, or nobody does.

## Why writing and not voice

The choice is deliberate, and there are three reasons.

An appointment is structured information. Service, person, day, time. On screen the customer sees the free times and taps one. On the phone, even with a human, there is a conversation of the kind "Tuesday? no, Wednesday? morning or afternoon?". Writing is simply faster for this job.

Writing is not misunderstood. The customer chose "Thursday 17:30" with a finger and has it written in the SMS. There is no "I thought you said Friday".

And the customer prefers it more than we think. Many people, the younger ones especially, avoid phone calls. They book from the bus, from the office, at midnight, without speaking to anyone.

## So what about the phone

It does not vanish. It changes role. Most calls to a salon or a medical office are four: "have you a gap?", "what time am I?", "I want to change", "I want to cancel". All four are now done without a call.

What remains are the calls that deserve a human: the question that is not simple, the customer who wants advice, the one who does not get on with mobiles.

## How you turn customers from the phone to the link

The link must be wherever the number is. On your map profile. On social media. On the card. In the window, as a QR code.

On the voicemail, a short message: "For appointments at any hour, open the link on our page." You record that, in your own voice.

When you pick up and the customer wants an appointment, you can make it from the same page on their behalf. They get their SMS, and next time they know how it is done.

And when you find a missed call, a written message with the link is often better than a call back that will also land on voicemail.

## Calls outside opening hours

The biggest gain is there. When the shop is closed, the phone is not answered, and the customer who wanted to book at that moment often does not ring again. We have written about that in [why businesses lose customers after hours](/en/articles/why-businesses-miss-leads-after-office-hours).

The booking page is open when you are not. It does not answer the call. It makes the call unnecessary.

## What else it is not

It is not a marketplace with many businesses where the customer compares prices. It is your own page.

It is not a general chatbot that chats about anything. The conversation has one purpose: to end in an appointment.

## Three cases

1. The owner of a beauty institute expected the system to answer the phone. She learns that it does not, puts the link on the voicemail, and calls about appointments drop.
2. A customer calls at nine in the evening, nobody answers, he hears the message and books from the link in two minutes.
3. An elderly customer phones as always. The hairdresser makes the appointment from the page on her behalf, and she gets the reminder by SMS.

## What you gain from written booking

- Simple calls about appointments fall
- Every appointment is in writing, with the time the customer chose
- Customers book when you cannot pick up
- The phone is left for what really needs a human

## How you fit it in beside the phone

1. For one week, note the reason for every incoming call.
2. Put the booking link wherever you print the number today.
3. Record a voicemail message that mentions the link.
4. Make phone appointments from the same page.
5. Count the calls again after a month.
