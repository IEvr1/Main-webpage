**Short answer:** Yes. In [Online Food Orders](https://www.nexaipla.com/foodorder/) the customer can send the order now and set a pickup or delivery time for later. The order enters the kitchen queue with its time written on the card, so that it is prepared when it should be. The customer gets a confirmation SMS with a link, from which they change or cancel it until then. Homepage: [NexAIpla](https://www.nexaipla.com/).

Demand for food is shaped like a mountain. The shop cannot change the hour at which people get hungry. It can, however, change the hour at which it learns what they will eat, and that makes a great difference to how the shift goes.

## What a scheduled order is

It is an ordinary order with one extra item: the time the customer wants it for. Instead of "as fast as possible", it says "at a quarter past one" or "at half past eight in the evening".

The customer sends it when it suits them. At eleven in the morning from the office, for lunch. In the afternoon from the sofa, for the evening. After that there is nothing to remember.

## Who uses it

Office workers, who know they will eat at one and do not want to lose fifteen minutes of their break waiting.

Parents, who know the children get back from their lesson at seven and want the food on the table at a quarter past.

Those who are expecting people. Six friends are coming for the match at a quarter to nine, and the pizzas must arrive at ten to.

And those who know that on Saturday evening the wait is long and want to secure their time.

## What the kitchen sees

The scheduled order appears in the same live queue as the rest, but its card states the time it is meant for. The kitchen knows it exists, without needing to cook it straight away.

That gives the shift leader something the phone never gives: a picture of the future. At six in the afternoon they know that for half past eight there are already nine orders. They can prepare ingredients, call in the second driver, decide whether more can be handled.

## When the cooking starts

That is decided by the kitchen and not by the software. The system shows the time. The cook knows that the pizza needs twelve minutes and the kleftiko two hours.

One simple rule helps: for every scheduled order, count backwards from the customer's time. For delivery, subtract the trip. For pickup the food should come out a few minutes before the customer arrives, not twenty, because it will go cold on the shelf.

Whoever is in charge of the screen looks at the scheduled ones at the start of every hour. It is a ten-second habit.

## The customer who changes their mind

The longer the interval between ordering and eating, the likelier it is that something will change. One guest is not coming. The meeting ran over.

The customer has the link in the SMS. From there they modify or cancel the order without phoning. The change shows on the card before anything goes on the heat.

## Hours and closures

The customer schedules within the frame you have set. When you declare a closure for a public holiday or for your own leave, the shop does not accept orders for that period. That is why it is worth entering closures in good time and not on the last day.

Times are calculated in the time zone of the country the shop is configured for, so "19:30" means the same to the customer and to the kitchen.

## What to expect

Scheduled orders will not become the majority. In many shops they are a small part. But they are the part that moves work from the peak of the mountain to its slope: ingredients cut early, dough ready, drivers who know what is waiting for them.

And they are often the largest orders. Whoever plans ahead is usually planning for many.

## Three scheduled orders

1. 10:50: an office sends six lunches for collection at 13:00. The kitchen prepares them before the lunchtime peak begins.
2. 17:20: a family asks for delivery at 19:15. The driver knows two hours ahead.
3. 18:00: a group orders pizzas for 20:50 and at 19:30 adds one more from the SMS link.

## What the shop gains

- The kitchen knows part of the peak before it starts
- Preparation is done in the quiet hours
- The customer secures their time without waiting on the line
- Changes up to mealtime are made by the customer

## How you fit them into the shift

1. Decide who checks the scheduled cards and how often.
2. For the main dishes, write down how many minutes ahead they must start.
3. Enter the coming weeks' closures in the system.
4. Tell customers, on the page and on the packaging, that they can order for later.
5. After a month, see which hours are scheduled most.
